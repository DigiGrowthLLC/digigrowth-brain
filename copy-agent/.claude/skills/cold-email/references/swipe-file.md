# Cold Email Swipe File

Source material Dylan supplied (2026-09-24): proven appointment-setting cold emails from a
pay-on-results gym/local-business marketing agency (author signs as "Charlie"; one example is
from Baudouin Borghans / NorthFlow Consulting). Use these as **structural models**, not text to
paste — see SKILL.md's "Adapting the swipe file" section for what must change before any of it
goes out under DigiGrowth's name (fabrication-risk lines are flagged inline with ⚠).

Two families:
- **'On' Framework** — longer, offer-forward. Leads with a compliment, a specific result claim,
  and a pay-on-results guarantee, then a Loom and a call ask.
- **'Off' Framework** — shorter, softer. Leads with a question ("taking on new customers?") or a
  referral/inquiry angle; the offer is implied, the Loom does the explaining.

---

## Subject lines

Simple subject lines that invoke curiosity work best.
- Quick question for (FULL NAME)
- Query for (FULL NAME)
- Question for (FULL NAME)
- Enquiry for (FULL NAME)
- For the attention of (FULL NAME)
- (FULL NAME)

^ replace (FULL NAME) with (COMPANY NAME) if unable to find name.

## Patterns worth keeping (distilled)

1. **Compliment first, specific to them** — "loving the look of (BUSINESS)" lowers defenses before the pitch.
2. **One concrete result + one avatar** — "(RESULT) per month with (AVATAR)", not "grow your business."
3. **Risk reversal up front** — pay-on-results / "if we don't deliver, you don't pay" appears early and again in the P.S.
4. **Human proof via video** — a Loom recorded for that prospect is the strongest "not a mass email" signal there is.
5. **Discovery framing, not a hard sell** — "learn more about your business, challenges & goals to see if there's a fit."
6. **One low-friction ask** — "call this week?", "reply yes", or "how many could you take on per month?"
7. **P.S. restates the single most important point** (usually the guarantee).
8. **Curiosity subject lines** — short, name-addressed, look like a 1:1 email rather than marketing.
