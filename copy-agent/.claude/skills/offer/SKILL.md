# Offer Skill

Design or improve an irresistible offer for any business — DigiGrowth or a client/prospect.
Sources:
- Jason Fladlin's "How To Create Killer Offers — 8 Secrets" (the 8 levers are captured inline below)
- Alex Hormozi's Grand Slam Offer framework ($100M Offers)

---

## Trigger

Use this skill whenever Dylan asks to:
- build a new offer (for DigiGrowth or another business)
- improve/critique an existing offer that isn't converting
- design a guarantee, bonus stack, or pricing structure
- figure out how to make a product/service harder to say no to

---

## Core Principle

People don't buy the best product — they buy the offer that best "speaks the value language" of
how their brain actually makes decisions. A killer offer isn't just a discount; it's engineered
using specific psychological levers, built on top of a structure that maximizes value.

This skill has two layers:
1. **Grand Slam Offer construction** (Hormozi) — the structural framework for what to build.
2. **The 8 Levers** (Fladlin) — the psychological levers for how to make each piece land harder.
   A great offer runs its problems/solutions through the Value Equation, then stacks 2-4 of the 8
   levers on top.

---

## Value Equation

```
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement)
        ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
               (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)
```

Maximize value by pushing the numerator up and the denominator down:
- **Dream Outcome ↑** — sharpen the transformation being sold (see below — not the service itself)
- **Perceived Likelihood of Achievement ↑** — proof, guarantees, case studies, done-for-you delivery
- **Time Delay ↓** — faster path to the first win and to the end result (overlaps with Lever 6, Better/Off Quicker)
- **Effort & Sacrifice ↓** — remove steps, decisions, and friction from buying and using it (overlaps with Lever 7, Hack Effort)

Every problem/solution/bonus/guarantee decision below should be run through this equation: does it
move a variable in the right direction, and by how much?

## Grand Slam Offer Construction

Build the offer in this order:

1. **Dream Outcome** — the specific result the customer wants, stated as the *transformation*, not
   the service. ("Own a home in a walkable neighborhood before your lease renews," not "real estate
   agent services.")
2. **Problems list** — brainstorm every problem/friction point standing between the prospect and
   that outcome, across all three phases: **before** purchase (trust, cost, time to decide),
   **during** delivery (effort, complexity, confusion), and **after** (maintenance, results fading,
   buyer's remorse).
3. **Solutions list** — for each problem, list what would actually solve it.
4. **Delivery vehicles** — decide *how* each solution gets delivered: 1:1, group, self-serve/DIY,
   done-for-you, software/automation, etc. Pick whichever is cheapest to deliver and most convenient
   for the customer — those two aren't in tension as often as they seem (self-serve/automated
   options are usually both).
5. **Bonuses** — solutions to problems that don't make the cut for the core offer become bonuses.
   Each bonus gets its own name and its own stated standalone value (a price tag, even if never
   sold alone) — this is what makes a bonus stack read as a pile of real value instead of filler.
   This is the same move as Lever 2 (Juice the Deliverables) — bonuses sourced from complementary
   partner businesses cost nothing to deliver and stack the fastest.
6. **Guarantee** — reverse the risk. Pick a type:
   - **Unconditional** — no-questions money back
   - **Conditional** — money back if specific conditions are met (e.g., did the work, didn't get the result)
   - **Anti-guarantee** — explicitly no refunds, for scarcity/exclusivity plays (rare, use carefully)
   - **Performance-based** — pay only on/after the result, or reimbursement of a downstream cost
     (this is Lever 4, Safe Is Sexy — the guarantee should cover the buyer's *total* risk, not just
     the sticker price)
7. **Scarcity/Urgency** — limit quantity (cohort size, spots, inventory) or time (deadline, bonus
   expiry, price increase) to force a decision instead of "I'll think about it."
8. **Naming** — name the offer/product using the **M-A-G-I-C** formula:
   - **M**agnetic reason why (why this exists / why now — ties to Lever 5, Reason Why)
   - **A**vatar (who it's for)
   - **G**oal (the outcome, not the process)
   - **I**nterval (the timeframe)
   - **C**ontainer/Method (the vehicle or proprietary-sounding system name)

## Pricing

Price based on **value delivered**, not cost of delivery. Anchor high, then justify the price with
the stacked value of the core solution plus every bonus (each with its own stated value from step 5
above) — the sum should make the asking price look like a fraction of the total value on the table.
This is the same move as Lever 1 (Short-Circuit the Anchor): don't price against competitors' cost
structure, price against the value of the transformation.

## The 8 Levers

### 1. Short-Circuit the Anchor
Every market has a price anchor people already expect (ChatGPT/Claude/Copilot/Google AI Pro all
converged on $20/mo). Don't compete by selling the same thing at that anchor price — sell something
**overwhelmingly more valuable at the same anchor price**. Example: bundle access to multiple
competing tools for the price of one, and solve the "which one do I use when" problem for the
customer.
- **Ask:** What's the unspoken price anchor in this market? Can we deliver 3-5x the value at that
  same price point instead of competing on price?

### 2. Juice the Deliverables (Bonus Stacking via Partnerships)
Find complementary businesses who will give you something valuable to bundle in — at no cost to
you — because it drives them business too (Amex + Waldorf Astoria: Amex gets loyalty, Waldorf gets
room-service/spa/valet spend). The bonuses can end up being worth more than the core product.
- **Ask:** Who has a business that's complementary (not competitive) to this one? What win-win deal
  can be made so their product/service becomes a bonus that costs us nothing?
- Real example from Fladlin: $9.8M in 8 days was driven largely by third-party bonuses worth more
  than the core offer.

### 3. Shrink the Market (Niche Down to Get Provable Results)
Broad, generic results are weak. The narrower the niche, the more specific and provable your
results become, and the more the market rewards it (Facebook: Harvard students only → global;
Airbnb: conference air-mattress renters → global; WhatNot: Funko Pop auctions → $8B/yr).
- **Ask:** What's the overlap between "what this market will pay for" and "what competitors refuse
  to specialize in"? Can we own a narrow slice with disproportionate results, then expand from proof?

### 4. Safe Is Sexy (Trust + Risk Reversal)
Buyers don't just weigh price — they weigh the risk of losing. Minimize perceived risk:
- **Resale/residual value** framing (Birkin bags: buyers justify $15K because they *could* resell)
- **Money-back guarantees** (a course guarantee that pays out to only ~5% of buyers doubled
  conversion)
- **Performance guarantees** that cover the *total* cost of buying, not just the product price
  (e.g., "run ads exactly as we describe — if you don't get positive ROAS, we reimburse your ad
  spend up to $X")
- **Ask:** What's the actual full cost of a "wrong" purchase for this buyer (price + wasted time +
  opportunity cost)? What guarantee removes that entire risk, not just the sticker price?

### 5. Reason Why (Make the Deal Believable)
An unbelievable deal needs a *reason* the buyer accepts, or skepticism kills the offer. A generic
"too good to be true" price triggers distrust; an explained/plausible reason removes it (a
liquidation reason for a discounted sofa; explaining the referral/affiliate incentive up front so
it reads as aligned self-interest rather than a trick).
- **Ask:** Why can we actually offer this? State the real (or plausible, transparent) reason
  explicitly in the copy — don't just assert the deal, justify it.

### 6. Better/Off Quicker (Time-to-Value Beats Perfection)
Time is more valuable than money. The faster a buyer can get *any* win, the easier the offer sells
— even if the result quality is mediocre (OpusClip: fast, "not really" great, still a $200M+
company because it beats the slow/expensive alternatives).
- **Ask:** What's the fastest possible path to a visible win for the buyer, even a small one? Can we
  front-load a fast result before the "real" deliverable finishes?

### 7. Hack Effort (Reduce Mental, Emotional, and Physical Friction)
Three types of buyer effort to strip out:
- **Mental** — progressive disclosure (Typeform: one question per screen instead of a giant form),
  remove clutter/decisions, make "what to do next" obvious
- **Emotional** — remove shame/frustration triggers (Netflix killing late fees removed
  blockbuster-style shame), lean into positive emotions (inspiration, hope, courage, respect)
- **Physical** — fewer clicks/taps/scrolls (Tinder's swipe vs. multi-field forms)
- **Ask:** Where in the buying/using journey are we forcing extra thinking, extra bad feelings, or
  extra clicks/taps that aren't necessary? What's the one-question-at-a-time version of this?

### 8. Status Management
Nearly every product either enhances or depletes the buyer's status. People will pay a premium for
things that make them look good and avoid things that make them look bad, even subconsciously
(Apple vs. "boring PC," Amazon selling vs. "nerdy" SEO work, GitHub's contribution graph turning
commits into a status flex).
- **Ask:** Does buying/using this make the customer look better to people who matter to them? Can we
  attack the identity/status of the alternative (not just its features)? Is there a visible/shareable
  status artifact we can build in (a badge, a stat, a public marker of progress)?

## Workflow

1. **Gather context.** If building for a client/prospect business, confirm: what they sell, price
   point, target market, current offer (if any), and why it's currently underperforming. If it's for
   DigiGrowth, pull context from `content-agent/context/brand-strategy.md`.
2. **Run the Grand Slam construction steps.** Dream Outcome → Problems → Solutions → Delivery
   Vehicles → Bonuses → Guarantee → Scarcity/Urgency → Name (M-A-G-I-C). This is the skeleton of
   the offer.
3. **Apply the 8-lever checklist on top.** For each lever, note whether it applies and how — most
   naturally slot into a specific construction step (Lever 1↔pricing, Lever 2↔bonuses, Lever
   3↔dream outcome/avatar, Lever 4↔guarantee, Lever 5↔naming/pricing justification, Lever
   6/7↔delivery vehicles, Lever 8↔dream outcome/naming). Not all 8 apply to every offer — pick the
   2-4 with the highest leverage for this business.
4. **Price it.** Anchor high, stack the value of the core solution + named bonuses, price against
   that stacked value rather than cost of delivery.
5. **Draft the offer.** Write the actual offer language: name, core deliverable, bonus stack,
   guarantee, price framing, scarcity, and the "reason why" if the deal looks too good.
6. **Stress-test against the Value Equation.** Does this raise Dream Outcome or Likelihood of
   Achievement, and lower Time Delay or Effort/Sacrifice, more than the alternative the buyer is
   comparing it to? Would a skeptical buyer believe it? Is there a faster path to a first win?
7. **Save.** Output to `outputs/offer-[business-name]-YYYY-MM-DD.md` with sections: Dream Outcome,
   Problems/Solutions/Delivery, Bonus Stack, Guarantee/Risk Reversal, Scarcity, Name, Price &
   Anchor Framing, Reason Why (if needed).

---

## Reminders

- Don't sell the same thing at the same price as competitors — sell more value at the anchor price,
  or the same value faster/safer/with less effort.
- A guarantee should cover the buyer's *total* risk (price + time + opportunity cost), not just
  refund the sticker price.
- Bonuses sourced from complementary-business partnerships can be worth more than the core product
  and cost nothing to deliver.
- Stack levers — the strongest offers combine risk reversal + speed + a status win, not just one
  lever in isolation.
