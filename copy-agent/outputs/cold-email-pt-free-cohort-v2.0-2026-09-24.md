Delivery: manual / cold-email tool with custom variables. Placeholders: `{{first_name}}`,
`{{company}}`, `[CUSTOM OPENER]`, `[LOOM LINK]`. (Email Handoff can resolve `{first_name}`,
`{business}` and `{loom}`, but it has no merge field for a custom opener yet.)
Offer: outputs/offer-mobile-pt-testimonial-2026-07-30.md (no management fee, 10-20 booked
consultations in 30 days, keep working free until at least 10 are booked, PT only pays ad spend to Meta)
Frameworks used: On 1.0 (T1-A, Dylan's chosen frame), v1.0 short Touch 1 kept as T1-B, Off 3.0 (T2, T3)
Change log:
- v1.0: initial (short On 2.0-style Touch 1)
- v2.0: Touch 1 rebuilt on the On 1.0 framework. The custom opener replaces the generic
  "love the look & feel" compliment. v1.0's Touch 1 is kept as Variant B for an A/B test.

_(words: ~130 + opener · links: 1 (Loom))_

### [CUSTOM OPENER] format
The opener finishes the sentence "...and noticed ___." so each value must be:
- a lowercase fragment starting with "you" or "your", with **no period at the end**
- one specific, real fact from their site, Google profile or reviews
- a plain fact with no praise. The next sentence ("That's exactly the kind of practice we want to partner with...") turns the fact into the reason you're reaching out, so the opener leads straight into the offer.

| Custom opener value | Reads as |
|---|---|
| `you offer a free consultation and full 60-minute sessions` | "...and noticed you offer a free consultation and full 60-minute sessions. That's exactly the kind of practice we want to partner with, and it's why you came to mind..." |
| `you do in-home visits across all of North Austin` | "...and noticed you do in-home visits across all of North Austin. That's exactly the kind of practice we want to partner with..." |
| `you just opened a second clinic in Round Rock` | "...and noticed you just opened a second clinic in Round Rock. That's exactly the kind of practice we want to partner with..." |
| `your reviews keep mentioning how much time you spend one-on-one` | "...and noticed your reviews keep mentioning how much time you spend one-on-one. That's exactly the kind of practice we want to partner with..." |

### What changed from the swipe-file On 1.0, and why
- **"Love the look & feel"** became your custom opener. A real, specific fact shows you did the research better than a general compliment does.
- **"XX (RESULT) with (avatar)... every month"** became 10-20 consultations in 30 days with people looking for PT, which matches the offer's 30-day sprint.
- **"100% pay on results / you don't pay"** became "completely free, and we keep working at no cost until we book 10." That's your real guarantee. "No money out of your pocket" was dropped because the practice does pay ad spend to Meta. The P.S. names the ad spend up front, so "completely free" stays honest.
- **"This isn't an automated message, I'm a real human"** was cut. These are sent from a tool on a schedule, and a prospect who sees Touch 2 arrive on time would know the claim was false. The video does the human part.
- **"We've already helped XX (NICHE) get (RESULT)"** became the case-study reason why ("we're taking on 3 PT practices as case studies"). There's no real PT result to cite yet, and this line also explains why the offer is free.
- The call ask is a reply ("open to a quick call?") with no booking link, so the Loom stays the only link.

## Self-check
- Touch 1 (A) is about 140 words with the opener, above the skill's 50-125 target because On 1.0 is the long frame. That's why Variant B is there to test against it.
- One link in Touch 1 (the Loom) and none in Touches 2-3.
- "Free" appears once (T1-A). Everything else says "no cost" or "no management fee."
- No borrowed proof, no invented stats, no false "not automated" claims. The guarantee matches the offer file.
- Touch 2 discloses the ad spend.

## Before sending, confirm
- **"3 practices" / "room for one or two more"** — only keep these if the limit is real right now.
- **Whether the custom openers match the format above** — lowercase, starting with "you"/"your", no period at the end.
