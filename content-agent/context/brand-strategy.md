# Personal Brand Strategy

Reference knowledge for building Dylan's personal brand and creating content. Distilled from study of brand-building principles.

---

## What a Brand Actually Is

- A brand is an **association between two things** — you and a topic, outcome, or feeling
- Branding is just teaching people to associate you with something valuable
- **3 elements of brand strength:**
  1. **Influence** — does seeing your name/face change people's behavior?
  2. **Directionality** — does it move them towards you or away?
  3. **Reach** — how many people have that association with you?
- You are always the purest version of your brand — lean into more of you, not less
- Be careful who you appear next to — audiences associate you with whoever shares your stage

## The 3 Stages of Building a Personal Brand

Hormozi's sequence: **Do Epic Shit → Associate → Defend the Belt.** Run stage 2 as a loop for years; add stage 3 once there's something to defend.

### Stage 1 — Do Epic Shit (earn the right to an opinion)

- Everyone skips the *personal* part: real skills, accomplishments or takes worth watching. No one cares about your opinion until they know it's worth something
- **The proof is the pudding.** 6 months of content on top of 10 years of experience beats 10 years of content on top of 6 months of experience
- **Two ways to earn credibility:**
  1. **Outcome** — a big accomplishment
  2. **Volume of work** — do so much work that watching the lessons is a bargain (100 dates → a 12-minute video = "two seasons of The Bachelor in 12 minutes")
- Educational content sells **time savings.** People want the CliffNotes, but you still have to write the book to produce the CliffNotes. "They don't want to go through 5 years of entrepreneurship. They want the lessons in 5 minutes."
- If your work is complicated or slow to pay off, **show the work:** 1,000 outreach attempts or 10,000 doors knocked, documented. People happily watch someone else do the work and take the lessons

### Stage 2 — Associate yourself with positive things, over and over

- "Positive" means what **your ICP** finds positive, so you must know exactly who you're going after
- Association strength = **frequency.** One podcast appearance is a weak pairing; a recurring co-host is a strong one
- **Visual associations** come from doing the same thing every day (Hormozi's beard, hat and flannel; Jobs's black turtleneck; Superman's S). Nothing about the item is unique; the repetition is
- **Non-visual associations (the 2.0 level):** pair your brand with what your audience likes, in three ways:
  1. Make **content** about the thing they like
  2. Make **products** (free and paid) they like
  3. Appear **next to the people** they like
- **Pick an avatar that is:** growing, in pain, able to afford you, and easy to find (algorithms now handle most of "easy to find")
- **No association is good or bad on its own.** It only matters whether your ICP likes it:
  - Bud Light + Dylan Mulvaney went viral, but the core audience disliked the pairing → revenue loss. Repaired by pairing hard with UFC
  - Coca-Cola's AI Christmas ad backfired: AI has its own brand (job cuts, layoffs), and a mass-market "happiness" brand inherited it. Nvidia or Microsoft could run the same ad fine
  - Apple's "human touch" ad landed because mass-market brands must serve mass-market wants
- After ~100 cool associations, you can show up alone and the cool points still rub off. That's when you've become a brand
- **Virtuous cycle of branding:** what *you* say → what *customers* say → what the *new buyer* concludes after buying. The product has the final word (trailer → friend's review → your own verdict on the movie), so the product must fulfill the content's promise
- Then: **keep looping for ~5 years and don't give up**

### Stage 3 — Defend the Belt (stay relevant)

- Big brands rarely lose head-on. Like Rome to the barbarians, they get **nibbled at the borders** by smaller players that are more relevant to niche audiences
- To stay on top, you need recurring **belt moments**: aspirational, public spectacles your audience finds cool (Red Bull's space jump, Apple keynotes, Tesla robotaxi/Optimus reveals, Hormozi's book launches and his ~$50M company sale, AI model launches)
- **Make big goals public and dated.** A shot across the bow you can't cancel aligns and energizes the team (why Jobs and Musk announce insane timelines)
- **Stakes create attention.** Every reality show opens with the prize money. Real stakes inside the company (humiliation, missed goals, lost money) make you and the goal interesting to people outside it
- You're only as good as your last season. Sports stay relevant because a new championship starts days after the last one ends

### Applying It to DigiGrowth

- **Stage 1 for Dylan = volume of work, documented.** Cold calls dialed, SMS and emails sent, outreach videos recorded, landing pages and blueprints built, clinic results as they come in. "I did X so you don't have to" content fits the Manufactured topic bucket below
- **Stage 2:** pair consistently with what independent PT owners value (full evaluation schedules, cash-pay patients, owning their practice, not depending on referrals) and appear next to people they respect (respected PT owners, PT business podcasts, client testimonials)
- **Watch the AI association.** For a skeptical, non-technical PT audience, AI can carry "replacing people" baggage. Lead with patients booked and the outcome, not the tech (matches the "never explain how it works" rule for prospect copy)
- **Stage 3 later:** public, dated goals with real stakes (e.g. the $10k/month target) documented in public as belt moments

## Organic Content Strategy

- **Early stage:** content = lead nurture. Don't worry about views. As audience grows, it becomes acquisition.
- **No CTAs inside content** — they kill reach and feel off. Put CTAs in: description, link in bio, and paid ads only.
- **Give:Ask separation** — content is ALL give. Ads are ALL ask. Never try to balance a give/ask ratio inside a single piece of content.
- **3:1 feed ratio** — for every ad someone sees from you, they should have seen 3 pieces of free value content first
- Paid ads protect the brand — they serve as the CTA vehicle while content stays pure

## Content Structure — The 3 P's Hook Framework

Every piece of content (video, post, article) should open with:

1. **Promise** — what will they get from this? What's the outcome?
2. **Proof** — why should they listen to you specifically? Establish credibility fast.
3. **Path** — show there's structure. Set expectations, then meet them.

The first 30 seconds are everything. Match the headline/thumbnail exactly with your opening words — if it says "5 ways to get clients," confirm that in the first sentence.

---

## Content Formats — Only 3 Exist

All content is some combination of:
- **Lists** — numbered or grouped points
- **Steps** — sequential process
- **Stories** — narrative examples and case studies

These can be intermixed freely (a list of steps, each with a story). Short-form = one of these. Long-form = many of these strung together.

---

## Topic Generation — 4 Buckets

Never run out of content by rotating between:

1. **Real-time** — capture ideas as they happen (tweet it, phone note, voice memo). These become everything else.
2. **Recent past** — review your calendar weekly. Every meeting, problem, or client situation is content.
3. **Far past** — big lessons, belief shifts, pivotal decisions that changed how you operate.
4. **Manufactured** — intentional challenges or experiments ("I did X for 30 days", "I started from scratch").

Build a repository where ideas live. Tweets → captions, scripts, reels, emails, posts. One idea, many formats.

## Volume vs. Quality

- Start with **quantity** to learn what works — you cannot know quality without reps
- **Power law:** 10x more time on one piece doesn't get 10x results — it can get 100x results
- As you scale, shift toward fewer, higher-quality pieces
- **Niche ratio:** 3:1 or 4:1 niche content to broad/lifestyle content — niche content gets actual customers, broad content expands reach

---

## Algorithm — Always Align With the Platform

- Every platform's objective: **keep people on the platform**
- They optimize for: **CTR → watch-through rate → watch-another rate**
- Hook drives CTR → completion drives distribution → rewatching drives recommendations
- Never rely on hacks (hashtag tricks, posting time tricks, etc.) — all platforms eventually close the loop
- Long-term strategy: make content people click on, watch all the way through, and immediately watch more of

---

## Framework Creation Process

When you have a repeatable insight worth packaging:

1. **Consume a large dataset** — watch your top content, interview customers, review your own results
2. **List everything** you observe across examples
3. **Find the commonalities** — what do they ALL have?
4. **Strip to essentials** — usually 3-5 things
5. **Make it memorable** — alliteration, acronym, or rhyme (Promise → Proof → Path)

---

## Scaling Content Production

- Start with an agency to buy lessons and stay accountable — speed > perfection early
- Transition in-house when your team's output quality beats the agency's
- Tweets/notes are the source of everything — they feed captions, scripts, posts, and reels
- One idea → tweet → caption → reel → email → blog = compounding leverage

---

## Controversy

- Controversy increases **reach only** — it doesn't improve influence strength or directionality
- Treat it the same as trending topics: useful for CTR, not a brand-building strategy
- If some people are turned away, that's fine — reach more people and provide more value to compensate
