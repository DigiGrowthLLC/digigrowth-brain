# Video Creation Skill

Produce a complete video template: script, visual direction, and B-roll ideas. Grounded in Expectations vs Reality psychology and all storytelling/hook frameworks.

---

## Trigger

Any message containing "video", "create a video", "video idea", "script", "write a script", "reel", "short form", "content piece", or "video template".

---

## Core Psychology — Expectations vs Reality

Every decision in this skill flows from one principle: **when reality beats expectations, viewers stay. When expectations beat reality, viewers leave.**

Expectations are set by the title, thumbnail, and first spoken line. Reality is what the video actually delivers. The job of the script is to set expectations just below what can be delivered, then exceed them — at the hook, at the first body point, and again at the end.

---

## Phase 1 — Intake

Ask questions **one at a time**, conversationally. Use each answer to inform how you phrase the next question. Skip any question already answered. Never ask for information already given.

**Question order:**
1. **Topic** — what is this video about?
2. **Platform** — based on the topic, suggest the most fitting platform(s) and ask which to target (IG Reel, TikTok, YouTube Short, YouTube long-form, LinkedIn)
3. **Target viewer** — use topic context to ask specifically (e.g., "is this aimed at fitness studio owners, or personal trainers just starting out?")
4. **Goal** — use the platform to frame options (e.g., for IG: follow, DM, share; for YouTube: subscribe, click link, book a call)
5. **Story angle** — offer 2-3 specific angle options based on everything learned so far. Let Dylan pick or redirect.
6. **Assets** — do you have talking-head footage or specific visuals, or is this concept only? (skip if already clear)
7. **CTA** — specific offer, resource, or next step to embed natively? (skip if goal already makes this obvious)

After each answer, acknowledge it briefly and move to the next question. Once all context is gathered, summarize what you have in one short block before moving to Phase 2.

---

## Phase 2 — Pre-Production

Work through these before writing a single line of script:

**1. Lock the title.**
The title defines what expectations the viewer brings in. You cannot write the intro without knowing what promise was made. Draft 2-3 title options and let Dylan pick, or confirm the one he gave.

**2. Confirm the story lens.**
Every topic has a common angle (most obvious take) and a rare angle (most specific, least told). The rare angle is what makes the video a category of one.
- Ask: what perspective has the fewest people told this from?
- Ask: what's the most counterintuitive or specific observation about this topic?
- Suggest 2-3 lens options if Dylan hasn't specified. Let him pick.

**3. Write the last line first.**
The last line is the most important — it gets remembered, shared, and replayed. For short-form, it should set up the first line to create a replay loop. Share it with Dylan before writing anything else. Confirm it's strong enough to stand alone.

**4. Outline the body points.**
Bullet the main points of the video. Then gut-check: are these actually unique, or do they summarize common knowledge? If they repeat what most people already know, stop and research until the points are genuinely novel. A script built on common knowledge cannot beat expectations.

## Phase 3 — Full Script

### Hook (written last in the process, but first in the video)

**5-step formula — all staccato (short) sentences:**

**Step 1 — Click Confirmation + Context Lean:**
First 1-2 lines. Three jobs: confirm the viewer clicked the right video (match the title's promise), give immediate topic clarity, and get them leaning in.

Ways to create the lean:
- Restate the conventional belief they already hold — they'll feel heard, which sets up the contrast
- Lead with a benefit or pain point they want solved
- Use a metaphor to simplify something complex
- Say something so interesting it immediately blows their mind
- Cult hopping: reference a known brand, celebrity, or cultural touchstone to wrap an unfamiliar idea in something already trusted

**Step 2 — Scroll Stop Interjection:**
One line. A stun gun. Contrasting word stops momentum and sets up the snapback. Use: *but, however, yet, although, therefore, on the other hand.* This line doesn't need to land — it just needs to stop them.

**Step 3 — Contrarian Snapback:**
The haymaker. Snap to the opposite direction of the initial lean — still on topic, but a completely different angle. Bigger shock = better hook. Must be a genuinely compelling insight. The formula is setup; it doesn't substitute for a real point.

**Step 4 — Proof:**
1-2 lines establishing why they should trust Dylan specifically on this topic. Results, reps, credentials. Brief — a claim without proof is a reason to leave.

**Step 5 — Plan:**
Tell them what's coming. An ordered list or number of steps. Sets structure expectations and gives them a reason to stay for all of it.

---

### Body

**Point ordering — second-best first:**
Never lead with the best point. Put the second-best body point first, best point second, third-best third.

Why: if point 1 is great and point 2 is slightly better, viewers stay expecting point 3 to be even better. If point 1 is the best and point 2 is slightly worse, the pattern reads as diminishing — they leave. Albums don't put the single first for the same reason.

**Value Loop — structure for each point:**
1. **Context** — what it is, as simply as possible. Surface-level facts first.
2. **Application** — how to do it. Specific, relevant example. Make it actionable enough that they could try it immediately.
3. **Framing** — why it matters and how it connects to the bigger picture. Zoom back out.

Pattern: zoom in for tactics, zoom out for context. Repeat. This wave creates comprehension while keeping people tracking the full arc.

**Re-hooking between points:**
Attention decays. Plant a mini-hook at the end of each body point that makes the viewer feel they must stay for the next one.
Format: confirm the value of the current point, then tease the next one as essential to make it work.
Example: *"That's the most important thing — but if you don't pair it with the next one, the whole thing falls apart."*

**Native embed (CTA):**
During outline, identify which body point naturally connects to a resource or next step. Weave it in without breaking flow:
- Option A: open the loop around a pain point → give the resource as the solve
- Option B: speed-run a framework so fast they couldn't fully absorb it → offer the resource as the implementation aid

Never announce the CTA — it should feel like a natural extension of the point.

---

### Outro

Leave on a high note — this is where likes, shares, and rewatches come from.
- Briefly summarize the points
- Remind them of the pain point solve — reinforce that reality beat their expectations
- End on a strong, memorable closer
- Think of it as the free chocolate at the end of a meal: it reframes the whole experience as great

---

### Tone
- Write as if talking to one specific person, not a crowd
- No "you guys", "everyone", "most people" — use "you"
- Conversational, direct. One friend talking to another friend.
- Read it out loud. Flag any sentence that sounds like a presentation or speech. Rewrite it as something you'd say sitting across a table.

### Rhythm
- Every sentence on its own line in the output
- Hook: short sentences only (staccato) — forces max clarity and increases value density per word
- Body + outro: mix short, medium, and long sentences. Right edge of the script should look jagged, not uniform.

### Speed to Value
- Short-form: 4-second timer starts at frame 1. Every word before second 4 must earn its place.
- Give a first hit of value inside the hook or immediately after it — never bury the best material.
- Structure: hook → first value hit → context → second value hit → continue
- Anything after someone clicks off is a black screen. Front-load relentlessly.

## Phase 4 — Visual Template

For every section of the script, add inline visual direction using `[VISUAL: ...]` callouts.

**Hook visual:**
- What's on screen in the first 3 seconds
- Motion direction (just enough motion — not too much, not too little; a subtle lean or quick cut can be enough)
- Text overlay: 3-5 words in big bold font that reinforces the context lean (distill the spoken line to its core)
- Rule: show while you tell — eyes process faster than ears

**Per body point:**
- B-roll idea or talking-head direction
- Any text overlays for key terms or stats

**B-Roll list (consolidated at the end):**
Every shot needed, described specifically enough to film or source, labeled by which section it's used in.

```
Platform: ...

Story Lens: ...
Goal: ...

[HOOK]
Spoken:
  Step 1 (Click Confirmation + Context Lean): ...
  Step 2 (Scroll Stop): ...
  Step 3 (Contrarian Snapback): ...
  Step 4 (Proof): ...
  Step 5 (Plan): ...
Text overlay: [3-5 words, bold]
Visual: [what's on screen + motion direction in first 3 seconds]

[SCRIPT + VISUALS]
[each sentence on its own line]
[VISUAL: inline callout for B-roll or on-screen direction]

[OUTRO]
[summary + pain point solve reminder + high note closer]

[LAST LINE]
[the closing line — strong enough to stand alone]

[B-ROLL LIST]
- [Shot description] — used in [Hook / Point 1 / Point 2 / Outro]
- ...
```

Save to `outputs/video-[topic]-YYYY-MM-DD.md`. Confirm what was saved.
