# DigiGrowth Positioning: The Digital Makeover (as of 2026-10-29)

How DigiGrowth frames its value on the website and in conversion copy. This file covers **framing
only**. Terms, prices and the guarantee live in `context/offer.md`, which wins on any conflict.
Offers vary per prospect, so swap in that client's agreed guarantee where it differs.

## The frame in one breath
The guarantee is the headline. The **Digital Makeover** is the named reason we can guarantee it,
and we never explain what's inside it. "A full marketing department for a fraction of the cost"
makes the makeover feel big without breaking it into parts. The prospect should leave every piece
of copy believing the result and curious about how we get it. The call is where they find out.

## Message hierarchy (always in this order)
1. **Promise:** 7-10 paid assessments within 30 days, guaranteed.
2. **Mechanism (named, never explained):** we give your practice a complete Digital Makeover.
3. **Value anchor:** everything a full marketing department would do, for a fraction of what it
   costs to hire one.
4. **Risk reversal:** if we fall short, we keep working, and you don't pay us a cent more until we
   get there.
5. **Curiosity close:** see what your practice's Digital Makeover would look like (book the call).

Short placements can drop steps 3-4, but never lead with anything other than the promise. The
makeover never appears without the guarantee next to it.

## Line bank
Headlines / hero:
- "7-10 paid assessments within 30 days. Guaranteed."
- "Your practice gets a Digital Makeover. You get 7-10 paid assessments within 30 days, guaranteed."
- "We don't sell marketing. We sell booked assessments, guaranteed."

The "how" (mechanism):
- "How can we guarantee it? Every practice we take on gets a complete Digital Makeover."
- "The Digital Makeover is how we guarantee your schedule fills."
- "Most clinics are one makeover away from a full evaluation schedule."

Value anchor:
- "Everything a full marketing department would do, for a fraction of what it costs to hire one."
- "A marketing department's worth of work, live in 7 days."
- "You're a PT, not a marketing director. The makeover takes that job off your plate."

Before/after (outcomes and feelings only, never deliverables):
- Before: "A schedule that rises and falls with referrals. A practice that's better in person than
  it looks online."
- After: "A full evaluation schedule you can count on. A practice that looks as good online as it
  is in the treatment room."

CTA:
- "See what your practice's Digital Makeover would look like."
- "Let's map out your makeover." (call booking)

## Why it works
- **Curiosity gap.** A named mechanism with its contents hidden invites "what's in it?", and the
  only place to get the answer is the call.
- **Mechanism belief.** A guarantee with nothing behind it reads as hype. A named makeover gives it
  a credible reason, and since nothing is listed, the prospect has nothing to nitpick or object to.
- **Anchoring.** "A full marketing department" makes the price feel small without itemizing anything.
- **Risk reversal.** The guarantee does the persuading; the makeover just makes it believable.
- **Transformation.** "Makeover" implies a before and after. Paint the after (full schedule,
  established-looking practice) and let them wonder about the steps.

## Vagueness rules
- **Never list or describe what's in the makeover** in prospect copy, the website included: no ads,
  AI, texting, calls/voice, social media, funnels, landing pages, reactivation, CRM, $49 or any
  price. Same rule as `never-explain-how-service-works`, applied to the makeover.
- Describe **outcomes and feelings** (full schedule, never missing a new patient, looking
  established online), not deliverables.
- **No numbers on the department claim** in public copy: no salary math, no "a tenth of the cost".
  Keep it "a fraction". If asked on a call, Dylan can explain the roles live.
- If a prospect asks "what's in the makeover?" by text or email, tease and redirect, in their own
  conversational context, not as a pasted script. Example beat: every practice's makeover looks a
  little different, and that's what he'd walk them through in a quick call.
- SMS lines: GSM-7 only (no em dashes, curly quotes or emoji).

## Where it goes
