# DigiGrowth ICP: Pain Points, Triggers, Objections and Language (as of 2026-11-22)

Voice-of-customer research for every piece of DigiGrowth marketing: cold SMS/email, the outreach
video, social posts, ads and the website. It covers **what the ideal prospect feels and says**. How
we frame the offer is `context/positioning.md`, and terms are in `context/offer.md`. Both win on any
conflict. Never paste these quotes into prospect copy as testimonials. They are language input.

## Sources analyzed

| Source | Volume | What it gave |
|---|---|---|
| Fireflies sales-call recordings | 18 recordings across 16 prospects (11 AI summaries + 7 transcripts filtered to the prospect's own lines). 1 more had no audio. These are every recording linked from an OS appointment; Fireflies' own meeting list only shows 1 (a Dec 2025 non-PT call) | Revenue, goals, constraints, objections, emotion |
| Per-prospect discovery forms ("Free Offer V.1.x Sales Script" docs) | 11 PT forms (5 more were gym/other niches, excluded) | Pricing, LTV, acquisition source, revenue, goal, "what's stopping you" |

## 1. Who the ICP actually is (from who bought vs. who didn't)

**Bought (4):** CrosaCore (Austin, cash chronic pain, $12-15k/mo), Pinnacle (Little Rock, hybrid
ortho + golf, ~$20k/mo), Boulder Body Solutions (cash MAT, ~$10k/mo), Advantage (Las Vegas, cash
manual PT, had just added a second PT).

What the buyers have in common:
- **An established practice, 2+ years old**, run by the owner who still treats full time.
- **Roughly $10-20k/month**, 1-2 clinicians, cash-pay or hybrid, with a specialty they're proud of
  (manual therapy, MAT, golf/TPI, chronic pain).
- **Open capacity with a name on it:** a new hire to fill, an associate they want to hire, or slow
  weeks they can point to.
- **A marketing scar:** ads that "flopped", SEO that plateaued, or referrals that dried up.
- **A spouse or partner in the decision** (Brian's wife runs the numbers, Phil's wife books, Lejoy
  has a business partner).

**Didn't buy, and why:**

| Profile | Example (from calls) | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Side practice under ~$6k/mo | PRI practice at $500/mo, mobile PT at $300/mo, golf PT at $3k/mo, mobile PT at $4-5k | "Cost is a barrier until I have a few more patients", "$600 + $1,500 may exceed my budget" |
| Brand new (under 6 months) | Concierge PT at 1 month, pelvic PT at 3 weeks | No database, "beyond current needs", "valuable for a larger practice" |
| Full or wait-listed | Dozens of SMS replies | "If I had 7-10 more assessments a month I'd need to hire another PT and that's a nightmare" |
| Over-committed owner | Concierge PT working 46-50 hrs/week | No room to see the patients even if they come |
| Insurance-based or part of a network | SMS replies | "It sounds like your offer is more targeted to cash pay clinics?" |

**The sweet spot:** an owner-operator, cash-pay or hybrid, $8-25k/month, 2+ years open, who has
just hired (or wants to hire) and now has a calendar to fill.

**Their patient, in their own words** (4 of 4 questionnaires plus most calls): 30-60, active or
a "work athlete", chronic or recurring pain that "failed PT, chiro and massage already", willing
to pay out of pocket. This is also the reason their own marketing struggles: that patient is
hard to find and hard to convince to skip their insurance.

## 2. Buying triggers (what makes "now" the time)

1. **Just hired or about to hire a PT.** This is the strongest trigger in the data: "My new hire
   PT starts in October", a sports PT with a former coworker starting in October who needs to be
   "as busy as possible", a runners' clinic that "just hired two people", and an owner who needs an
   associate to reach $40k. A new salary with an empty schedule is urgent.
2. **Lead flow suddenly dropped.** One cash-pay owner went from about 24 leads a month to 3-7:
   "Meta ads has dropped off significantly, probably by like a solid 80 percent". Revenue went from
   $15-21k to about $6k.
3. **A life event is coming:** a new baby ("Growth desired within six weeks, baby due October 18"),
   kids, wanting to "pay self back, spend time with kids".
4. **A slow season they can see coming:** the holidays, or "two weeks without patients".

## 3. Pain points, ranked by how often they came up

### P1. "Unpredictable": new patients rise and fall with referrals and word of mouth
On almost every discovery form, the answer to "consistent or unpredictable?" was **"unpredictable"**.
- "Referral flow inconsistent... month-to-month unpredictability"
- "Word-of-mouth referrals slowed over past six to eight months"
- "I'm filling my calendar basically day by day right now"
- "Fewer slow weeks" (stated motivation of a $20k/mo owner)
- Most acquire patients through word of mouth, events, workshops, networking, gyms and coaches.
  One owner does "events... meta ads... business networking meetings... workshops. I'm everywhere
  all the time," and it still isn't consistent.

**Use it:** this is the core before-state. It matches positioning.md's "a schedule that rises and
falls with referrals". Lead with the feeling ("you never know what next month looks like"), not
with the word "referrals" alone.

### P2. They've been burned by marketing: ads "flopped" and agencies brought junk
- "Google ads was a flop, typically Meta ads flopped the 3 times we tried" (Advantage)
- "We've tried facebook ads, google ads and they were not worth the money we spent" (Pinnacle)
- A previous provider brought "inquiries from outside area, other cities, and outside country"
- "I've tried some other things with social media and stuff, and that hasn't been as effective. So
  that was also costing me some money"
- "My Facebook ads aren't doing what they're supposed to be doing, even at the thousand dollar
  budget"
- "Just hire a content person and learn the Andromeda and f around and find out because I'm not.
  I'm tired of this."

**Use it:** "You've probably tried ads already" is a safe, true opener for this ICP. Our pay-for-
results structure ("you're not paying for clicks, you're paying for booked assessments") answers
the scar directly. A $20k/mo owner said of the model: "It's not the typical way it's done in
marketing but I think it's the way that should be done."

### P3. "Leads" aren't the problem. Cash-ready patients are.
This is the most important nuance in the whole dataset. To this ICP, "qualified" means **willing
to pay cash for care insurance won't cover.**
- "Booking 10 consults is nice, but booking 10 qualified consults willing to pay for these
  treatments that are not covered by insurance is totally different."
- "That's never really worked for me because I need people that are interviewed and willing to
  pay the price."
- "We are good with leads. It's preparing the lead to pay cash that's the key, so that it's not a
  wasted phone call."
- Drop-off reasons on the questionnaires: "Cost. Because we are cash-pay, some people hesitate"
  (CrosaCore), "Wants to use their insurance or don't have the money" (Advantage), "Price if we
  don't take their insurance" (Pinnacle).
- In CrosaCore's first round, 2 of 4 leads asked about insurance and dropped.
- A brand-new pelvic PT: "convincing patients to pay directly despite insurance premiums,
  deductibles..."

**Use it:** stop saying "leads" or "consults" in prospect copy. Say **paid** assessments, or
"patients ready to pay for care". The guarantee's word "paid" is the strongest thing in the offer
for this audience, so make it louder. This also reinforces the current $49 prepaid model (prepaid
= pre-qualified), which you'd mention only when the prospect asks.

### P4. The owner is the bottleneck: no time, and marketing isn't their thing
- "Lead generation and marketing are outside my wheelhouse", "procrastination, doesn't like
  marketing"
- "Time is available, but traditional marketing and social media are not appealing"
- Missed calls: "When people call me while I'm literally with a patient... I'll send a text
  message manually from my cell phone between clients."
- "I don't have much time for talking over the phone", "I work all day and take care of family at
  night", "I'm booked 13 hrs tomorrow"
- What's stopping growth (form answers): "Time, qualified prospect", "Capacity of the clients you
  can take on", "Procrastination", "Age of business", "Targeted marketing channels"
- Ideal future: "Running itself", "work less", "part-time hands-on and do more community stuff",
  "replace himself eventually"

**Use it:** "You're a PT, not a marketing director" lands. Concrete before-state images they gave
you: texting missed calls between patients, filling the calendar day by day, doing the marketing
at night. Don't describe our deliverables. Describe the after: the schedule fills while they treat.

### P5. They don't know their numbers
Customer acquisition cost was "No", "Nothing", "Time" or "don't track it" on nearly every form.
Lifetime value was known roughly ($500 to $9,000, most $1,500-3,000). They know their revenue and
what they want it to be.

**Use it:** the calculation close works because the prospect has never done the math. In content,
"one new patient a month pays for this" and "what's one patient worth to you?" are strong hooks.
Use their real LTV range ($1,500-3,000 for cash plans of care) in posts and on the site.

### P6. The money anxiety under the business talk
This only surfaced on calls, never in texts, and it's the deepest emotional layer:
- "I can survive at six. I'm surviving at six right now... but I shouldn't be worried about paying
  my freaking credit card."
- "How would you feel if nothing changes in 6-12 months?" Answers: **"Disappointed"** (three
  times), **"Defeated"**, **"Not fulfilling"**.
- "That is not okay" (after reading their own monthly lead numbers aloud), "I'm nervous to look."
- Goal reasons: "Kids and wife comfortable", "Kids, retirement", "Pay self back, spend time with
  kids", "Support my family and do what I want to do", "Proof of concept"

**Use it:** for social and video (story posts, Dylan-on-camera) rather than cold SMS. The frame:
"you didn't open a practice to worry about next month." Keep it empathetic, never fear-based.

### P7. Growing past themselves: hiring, the next location, the "real" vision
Most goals were **double current revenue** ($15k to $30k, $20k to $40k, $6k back to $15-20k,
$3-5k to $10k) so they can hire a PT, open a space, or step back to part-time treating.

**Use it:** "fill your new hire's schedule" is a great specific campaign angle for cold outreach
to practices that recently posted a PT job (a possible lead-gen filter: scrape job posts).

### P8. Patient retention is slipping (secondary)
"Even the people that I currently have... are kind of disappearing", switching from packages to
visit-by-visit "kind of screwed me over", a Texas board ruling paused packages. This is a pain for
reactivation and follow-up messaging, not a headline.

## 4. Objections, in their words, and what answers them

| # | Objection | Their words | What answers it |

There is always a catch", "I get contacted by a lot of scammers", "We get inundated with similar requests", "been in this game too long for a gimmick pilot program", "could you tell me what state your LLC is registered with?" | Be plainly straight: name, company, what they get, what it costs them. | O2 | **"Tell me what it is before I give you 20 minutes."** | "Can you give me a 30 second elevator speech? ## 5. What happens after the sale (promises to make or avoid)

These pains came from clients mid-campaign, and they decide renewal:
- **Lead quality and location:** "a lead booked from the ad but it looks like they live in
  Georgia", "weird inquiries", leads who "travel to Vegas so sessions will be very spaced out",
  "Kevin Hill... wanted to use insurance".
- **Did it pay for itself:** "I'm barely getting just the ad spend covered" (Advantage, before
  pausing). Clients judge on paid patients, not bookings.
- **Owner time:** "if I was able to take the google meets calls it would've been much better but
  just unrealistic with my time".
- **Calendar trust:** a double booking ("booked me a client at a time I am already seeing
  someone"), a confirmation time that didn't match the calendar, no-shows ("Neither one of the
  booked calls showed up today"), "I'm getting one of these reminders every ten minutes".
- **Responsiveness:** "Still waiting on a response since the 12th?"
- **Control over their brand:** Pinnacle asked to edit every confirmation and reminder, add
  directions and attire, and know "how will we be notified" about leads.

**Marketing implication:** the promises that win renewals are local, cash-ready patients who show
up, with no extra work for the owner and nothing going out under their name that they haven't
approved. Those are also the exact fears in O1-O6, so say them in prospect copy.

## 6. Language bank (their words, for copy)

Before-state:
- "unpredictable", "good and bad weeks", "slow weeks", "hit or miss"
- "filling my calendar day by day"
- "word of mouth slowed down", "referrals dried up"
- "ads flopped", "not worth the money", "tried it, didn't work"
- "a wasted phone call", "they wanted to use their insurance"
- "I'm everywhere all the time" (events, workshops, networking)
- "marketing isn't my thing", "outside my wheelhouse"
- "texting missed calls between patients"
- "disappointed", "defeated", "I'm tired of this"

After-state:
- "a full schedule", "consistent", "predictable", "running itself"
- "keep my new PT busy", "hire another PT", "move into a bigger space"
- "work less", "more time with my kids", "pay myself back"
- "patients who are ready to pay", "people who already know the price"
- "I just treat"

Words that build trust with this ICP: straight, real person, no catch, local, paid, qualified,
show up, you approve everything, pause anytime.

Words to avoid in cold copy: leads, consults, AI, bot, funnel, pilot program on its own (it reads as
"gimmick" without the guarantee next to it), "explode your practice", hype.

## 7. Decisions for Dylan (tensions the data surfaced)

1. **Never-explain vs. "tell me before I take the call".** The cold replies show a skeptical ICP
   that is tired of cold outreach and asks "what is it, where do they come from, what does it
   cost me?" before booking. 59 SMS threads (4% of replies) explicitly asked how it works or for
   details by email. The positioning rule is right about not listing deliverables, but the data
   says a **one-line answer to "what do I get and what does it cost me"** earns trust (for
   example: "We book local patients who've already paid for their first assessment onto your
   calendar. The first 30 days are free on our end, you only cover the ad budget."). Worth deciding
   how much of that the SMS/email setters may say.
2. **The disqualifier line.** The script says $15k+/month, but two of four buyers were around
   $10k. The real filter in the data is less about revenue and more about having **2+ years open
   plus open capacity plus the owner willing to spend about $1k on ads.** Side practices under
   $6k never closed.
3. **Gatekeepers.** 209 of 1,347 replied SMS threads (16%) were answered by a front desk, admin
   or AI receptionist, often with "email the owner at...". Practices with staff are the ones big
   enough to buy, so a gatekeeper path (a short emailable one-pager plus the personal video) is
   worth building, not treating as a dead end.
4. **Proof is the closing blocker.** Two of four buyers asked to talk to existing clients. A
   testimonial or reference from CrosaCore, Pinnacle or Boulder, with real paid-assessment
   numbers, is the highest-value marketing asset you don't have yet.
5. **New-hire targeting.** The strongest trigger (P7, trigger 1) is findable: practices that just
   posted a PT job on Indeed or LinkedIn. That could be a leadgen filter and a cold opener.

## 8. How to use this, by channel

**Cold SMS / email (setters, sequences):** open on P1 or P2 in one short line, put "paid" in front
of every number, answer O1 before they ask (real person, real company, no catch), keep AI out.
Example beats, not scripts:
- "Most cash-pay owners I talk to say new patients are unpredictable month to month. Is that you?"
- "Saw you're hiring a PT. Want help filling their schedule with paid assessments?"
- "Not a bot, not a catch: we only get paid when patients book and pay for an assessment."

**Social (Dylan's content):** P6 stories (the credit-card line, "disappointed"), P5 math posts
("what one cash patient is worth"), P2 myth-busting ("why your Meta ads stopped working"), P3 ("10
leads is not 10 patients"), and client proof as soon as it exists.

**Website:** keep the guarantee-led hierarchy from positioning.md. Add a before/after in their
words (P1, P4), a short "why it's paid assessments, not leads" block (P3), a "no catch" section
answering O1/O4/O6 (who we are, how you pay, you approve everything, pause anytime), and client
proof with real numbers (O5).

**Sales call:** the discovery questions already surface everything above. Add three: "What have
you tried for marketing, and what happened?" (P2), "Are you hiring, or planning to?" (trigger 1),
and "Who else weighs in on this decision?" (spouse or partner, in 3 of 4 closes).
