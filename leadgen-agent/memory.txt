NICHE DEFINITION:
Qualified = independently owned, single-location physical therapy practice. Mobile/in-home service is NOT required — a normal clinic/office-based practice qualifies. Focuses on general orthopedic, post-surgical, or geriatric patients. Owner-operated with a small team (1–6 staff). No in-house marketing team or agency.

Disqualified = corporate-owned or franchise PT clinics, any practice with more than one location, hospital-based or specialty-only clinics (e.g. sports-performance-only), university or teaching hospital PT programs, practices with active paid advertising or an in-house marketing coordinator.

CURRENT TARGET MARKETS:
All US states — priority on Sun Belt markets: Florida, Texas, Georgia, South Carolina, North Carolina, Arizona, Tennessee, Virginia

DISQUALIFIER SIGNALS (check website and social):
- Actively running Meta or Google ads
- Has a marketing coordinator or agency listed
- Corporate group ownership mentioned, or more than one location (a "Locations" page listing multiple cities/addresses, "our other clinics", etc.)
- Sports-performance-only or pediatric-only (narrow specialty focus)
- Hospital-based or specialty rehab center (no general orthopedic/geriatric care)

GOOD LEAD SIGNALS:
- Independent PT/DPT owner named on the site (Dr. [Name]'s Physical Therapy, [Name] PT & Wellness)
- Single physical address, no "locations" or multi-city language
- Website exists but minimal paid marketing evidence
- Google reviews but no recent social media posts (30+ days since last post)
- General orthopedic, post-surgical, or geriatric patients as primary focus

AGENCY CONTEXT:
COLD CALL OPENER CRITERIA:
The one-line opener feeds into this exact cold call framework:
"Hey [Name], this is Dylan — quick question for you. I was looking at [Practice Name], and I noticed [OPENER]."

The opener must complete that sentence naturally. It should sound like something Dylan genuinely noticed — not a compliment, not a pitch.

ARTICLE RULE: Always use "a" or "an" before the service, offer, or specialty. NEVER use "that", "the", or "your".
  ✅ "you offer a complimentary first evaluation"
  ❌ "you offer that complimentary first evaluation"
  ❌ "you offer your complimentary first evaluation"

Priority order for what to pull from the website:
1. A specific RESULT or OFFER they advertise (complimentary first visit, free consultation, new patient special)
2. A specific SERVICE or PROGRAM (post-surgical rehab, fall-risk assessments, senior mobility program, balance and gait training, dry needling, blood flow restriction training)
3. A specific NICHE or PATIENT TYPE (post-surgical only, senior/geriatric patients, sports/athletic patients)
4. A specific METHOD or APPROACH (one-on-one sessions with the same therapist every visit, no waiting room, same-day scheduling, cash-pay/out-of-network model)
5. If nothing specific exists — return null. Do not fabricate.

OPENER TONE RULES:
- Always sounds like Dylan noticed something specific, not like he's running a list
- Never complimentary — observational only
- One sentence only
- Use "a" or "an" before the service/offer/specialty — never "that", "the", or "your"
- If a specific offer, result, or specialty exists on their site, reference it
- Never mention the website, never say "I was browsing" or "I came across"
- Observation only — no question. Dylan handles the question on the call.

OPENER EXAMPLES (use these as style reference — observation only, no question):

✅ "you do one-on-one post-surgical rehab appointments"

✅ "you offer a free consultation for new patients"
✅ "you book a full hour with the same therapist every visit"
