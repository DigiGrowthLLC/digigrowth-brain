CHAIN / FRANCHISE BLACKLIST:

NICHE DEFINITION:
Qualified = independently owned mobile or in-home veterinary practice where house-call or in-home visits are the PRIMARY or a core service. Focuses on companion animals (dogs and cats). Owner-operated with a small team (1–6 staff). No in-house marketing team or agency.

Disqualified = corporate-owned or franchise vet clinics, brick-and-mortar-only practices with no mobile component, emergency/specialty-only animal hospitals, university or teaching hospitals, equine-only or exotic-only practices, practices with active paid advertising or an in-house marketing coordinator.

CURRENT TARGET MARKETS:
All US states — priority on Sun Belt markets: Florida, Texas, Georgia, South Carolina, North Carolina, Arizona, Tennessee, Virginia

DISQUALIFIER SIGNALS (check website and social):
- Actively running Meta or Google ads
- Has a marketing coordinator or agency listed
- Corporate group ownership mentioned (multiple locations under one brand)
- Brick-and-mortar only — no mention of mobile, house call, or in-home
- Exotic animal only or equine only (wrong animal focus)
- Emergency or specialty hospital (no general wellness/primary care)

GOOD LEAD SIGNALS:
- "House call", "in-home", "mobile vet", or "we come to you" on website or in business name
- Independent DVM owner (Dr. [Name]'s Mobile Vet, [Name] Veterinary House Calls)
- Website exists but minimal paid marketing evidence
- Google reviews but no recent social media posts (30+ days since last post)
- Suburban service area listed with radius or zip codes
- Companion animals: dogs and cats as primary patient focus

AGENCY CONTEXT:
COLD CALL OPENER CRITERIA:
The one-line opener feeds into this exact cold call framework:
"Hey [Name], this is Dylan — quick question for you. I was looking at [Practice Name], and I noticed [OPENER]."

The opener must complete that sentence naturally. It should sound like something Dylan genuinely noticed — not a compliment, not a pitch.

ARTICLE RULE: Always use "a" or "an" before the service, offer, or specialty. NEVER use "that", "the", or "your".
  ✅ "you offer a complimentary first in-home wellness visit"
  ❌ "you offer that complimentary first in-home wellness visit"
  ❌ "you offer your complimentary first in-home wellness visit"

Priority order for what to pull from the website:
1. A specific RESULT or OFFER they advertise (complimentary first visit, free new client consultation, new client special, wellness guarantee)
2. A specific SERVICE or PROGRAM (in-home euthanasia, Fear Free certified, senior pet wellness plan, anxiety-free home visits)
3. A specific NICHE or PATIENT TYPE (cats only, senior dogs, end-of-life care, house call only practice)
4. A specific METHOD or APPROACH (no waiting room, same-day scheduling, in-home exams, house call model)
5. If nothing specific exists — return null. Do not fabricate.

OPENER TONE RULES:
- Always sounds like Dylan noticed something specific, not like he's running a list
- Never complimentary — observational only
- One sentence only
- Use "a" or "an" before the service/offer/specialty — never "that", "the", or "your"
- If a specific offer, result, or specialty exists on their site, reference it
- Never mention the website, never say "I was browsing" or "I came across"
- Observation only — no question. Dylan handles the question on the call.

OPENER EXAMPLES (use these as style reference — observation only, no question):
✅ "you offer a complimentary first in-home wellness visit"
✅ "you specialize in senior dog and cat care"
✅ "you're Fear Free certified for in-home exams"
✅ "you do in-home end-of-life care and euthanasia appointments"
✅ "you focus exclusively on cats"
✅ "you offer a free new client consultation for new pet owners"
✅ "you cap appointments to allow a full in-home exam every visit"
✅ "you run a senior pet wellness plan for dogs over eight"
