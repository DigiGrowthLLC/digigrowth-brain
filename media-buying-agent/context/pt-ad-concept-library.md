# PT Ad Concept Library

A menu of ad concepts that independent physical therapy practices keep running on Meta. Use it when
choosing concepts for a client (`build-campaign-plan` step 6B, `generate-ad` inputs): match each
concept to the client's services, differentiators and target demographic, then adapt it under
our rules (bottom of this file).

**Offers:** "free consult, never a price" below is the *default*. If the client has an approved
offer from `build-offer` (the "## Offer" section of their `client-context.md`), every concept
sells that offer instead, prices included if it has one.

**What "proven" means here.** Researched 2026-10-08 via the Meta Ad Library connector (US, active ads),
plus the 2026-09-13 long-running ad scan in `ad-creative-principles.md` and published PT/chiro ad
guides. The connector mostly shows recently launched ads, not how long each has run. So most
concepts are proven by **convergence** (many separate PT clinics running the same concept now), and
only #6 (InMotion PT, live since April 2025) and #13-14 (long-running in the September scan) are
proven by longevity. For verbatim copy and run lengths, see `context/proven-ads/<vertical>.md`
(made by `research-competitors`). That file holds individual proven ads; this one holds
reusable concepts.

Each concept lists: what it is · real examples · **best for** (client fit and demographic) ·
funnel stage · how to adapt it for our clients.

## Offer concepts (what the ad asks for)

### 1. Free workshop on one condition
- **What:** a free in-person or online class about one problem (back pain, knee, shoulder or
  rotator cuff, foot and ankle, balance/neuropathy, golf performance, skier injury prevention).
- **Examples:** IMG PT "Rotator Cuff Workshop Reveals How to Get Back to Normal Without
  Medications, Injections and Surgery"; Panetta PT "FREE Back Pain Workshop"; Plymouth PT "FREE
  Golf Performance Workshop" and "Knee Pain Workshop"; Motus Rx "FREE Foot & Ankle Class"; GTS PT
  "Free Shoulder Workshop"; Village Fitness "FREE Low Back Pain Workshop, Adults 45+"; Total
  Recovery "FREE Peripheral Neuropathy Workshop"; Dynamic PT "FREE Injury Prevention Workshop for
  Skiers & Snowboarders"; Rebound PT "Everyday Tips for Balance, Safety & Injury Prevention."
- **Strongest evidence of any concept:** the most repeated, by far.
- **Best for:** clients willing to host an event (clinic space or Zoom); older adults 50+ (balance,
  arthritis, back) who like learning before committing; clinics with a strong educator-type owner.
- **Stage:** unaware / problem aware.
- **Adapt:** this is a *different offer* from the free consult. Only use it if the client agrees to
  host. The workshop's job is to lead into the free consult. Never a price.

### 2. Free discovery visit or call
- **What:** a no-cost first conversation with the PT to see if PT can help (the Paul Gough model).
- **Examples:** Factor PT "Request a FREE Discovery Visit"; Move Empower Concierge PT "FREE
  Physical Therapy Discovery Call."
- **Best for:** every client. It's our default offer (free consult). Strongest for skeptical or
  "PT didn't work before" avatars, and cash-pay clinics where people want to meet the PT first.
- **Stage:** product aware / most aware.
- **Adapt:** use as-is, framed as the risk reversal ("free, no obligation, you leave with a plan").

### 3. Diagnostic for one body part
- **What:** a thorough, specific assessment of one area, named like a product.
- **Examples:** Land and Sea PT "$59 Complete Shoulder Diagnostic - 60 Min Sports PT in North
  County"; Optimize PT "$97 Complete Shoulder Diagnostic"; Hart & Hustle "$97 Run Strong Analysis
  for Columbus locals"; chiro "$97 Knee Pain Evaluation."
- **Best for:** sports/performance clinics, runners and lifters, clinics with a signature
  assessment (gait analysis, a long first visit).
- **Stage:** solution aware / product aware.
- **Adapt:** **keep the structure, drop the price** (client-funnel rule). It becomes a named free
  consult ("Free 60-minute Shoulder Assessment", "Free Run Check"), using only real details of the
  client's first visit.

## Angle concepts (what the ad says)

### 4. "Without pills, injections or surgery"
- **What:** the non-invasive promise: fix it without drugs, shots or an operation.
- **Examples:** IMG PT (above); chiro "Finally Free From Back Pain - No Surgery"; repeated across
  PT, chiro and ortho in the September scan.
- **Best for:** avatars afraid of surgery or tired of pills: 45+, people told they "might need
  surgery," people with arthritis or a bad back.
- **Stage:** solution aware (PT versus the alternatives).
- **Adapt:** frame it as an option ("another option before surgery"), never a promise that surgery
  won't be needed. Check the client's voice rules for banned claims.

### 5. Split by age and gender
- **What:** the same ad run as separate versions for each segment.
- **Examples:** Village Fitness "Women 45+: Why Does Your Back Pain Keep Coming Back?" next to
  "Men 45+: ..."
- **Best for:** clients whose patients cluster in clear segments (e.g. women 45+ with back or
  pelvic issues, men 50+ who golf). It's the "personification" rule from the AdsMastery 2027
  section in practice.
- **Stage:** problem aware.
- **Adapt:** Meta's personal-attributes rule rejects "Women 45+ with back pain" if it asserts the
  viewer's condition. Make it about a situation: "Women 45+: why does the same ache keep coming
  back after the gym?" Use only age ranges the client actually treats (Crosacore: 60 and under).

### 6. "Why does it keep coming back?"
- **What:** a recurring-problem question hook. It names the frustration of a problem that never
  quite goes away.
- **Examples:** Village Fitness (above); **InMotion PT "Dealing with aches and pains?"**, the one
  ad in the research confirmed long-running (live since April 2025).
- **Best for:** chronic, recurring avatars: desk workers, runners with repeat injuries, people who
  tried rest, stretches or a previous PT.
- **Stage:** problem aware.
- **Adapt:** pair it with the unique-mechanism problem ("rest calms it down, it doesn't fix why it
  happens").

### 7. Bust a belief
- **What:** take away the excuse the reader uses.
- **Examples:** TheraWest PT "You're Not Getting Old. You're Hurt."
- **Best for:** 40-65 adults who blame age; also "it's just how my knees are" runners.
- **Stage:** unaware → problem aware.
- **Adapt:** a strong unaware-stage hook. Keep it kind, never mocking.

### 8. A sport or identity niche
- **What:** an ad for one activity group, in their language.
- **Examples:** Plymouth PT golf workshop; Dynamic PT skiers and snowboarders; Hart & Hustle "Run
  Strong"; Land and Sea "Sports PT."
- **Best for:** clients with a sport specialty or athletic patients (runners, golfers,
  pickleball, CrossFit/lifters, skiers), and local sports cultures (golf towns, running cities).
- **Stage:** problem aware / solution aware.
- **Adapt:** use insider words from `customer-language.md` (Connection, from the Creative Don
  section). Humor and identity work well for this avatar.

### 9. Convenience: PT at home
- **What:** the mechanism is *where* care happens, not just what it is.
- **Examples:** Move Empower Concierge PT "Physical therapy without the drive."
- **Best for:** clients who offer in-home or concierge visits (**Crosacore**), busy parents and
  professionals, older adults who don't drive or can't easily get out.
- **Stage:** solution aware (us vs. the drive-to-a-clinic routine).
- **Adapt:** only for clients who really do home visits. Crosacore leads with in-clinic (see its
  `client-context.md`), so home visits are the secondary angle there.

### 10. Empathy for fear of falling
- **What:** a soft, emotional hook about losing confidence on your feet.
- **Examples:** iMotion PT "If every step feels uncertain... you're not alone."; "You Can't Balance
  on Feet You Can't Feel" (neuropathy).
- **Best for:** clients who treat balance, vestibular or neuropathy patients, and seniors 65+.
  Adult children of aging parents are a second audience.
- **Stage:** problem aware.
- **Adapt:** the emotional mode from the Creative Don section (fear, gently). The hook describes
  the situation ("the stairs feel different lately"), never a diagnosis. Not for clients whose
  age cap excludes seniors.

### 11. Women's health / pelvic floor
- **What:** take the shame off a common, rarely discussed problem.
- **Examples:** several advertisers, e.g. "Pee leaks aren't 'normal'."
- **Best for:** clients with a pelvic health PT. Postpartum moms, women 35-60, runners who leak
  when they jump.
- **Stage:** unaware ("it's not normal, and it's fixable").
- **Adapt:** only if the client really offers it. Extra care with the personal-attributes rule:
  talk about the situation ("skipping the trampoline with the kids"), never "your incontinence."

### 12. "What the scan missed"
- **What:** imaging doesn't tell the whole story, a movement assessment does.
- **Examples:** "What my PT saw that the X-ray missed," "a bunion is not only a bone problem"
  (from product sellers using PT framing, not clinics).
- **Best for:** avatars who had an MRI or X-ray that "looked fine" but still hurt, or were told
  "nothing structurally wrong." Skeptical, research-minded patients.
- **Stage:** solution aware.
- **Adapt:** no medical claims. Present it as the PT's approach (watching how you move), backed
  by the client's own patient reviews.

## Format concepts (how it looks), from the 2026-09-13 long-running scan

### 13. City callout + condition checklist + one simple offer
- **What:** a "Hey Phoenix!" opener, a plain list of the conditions treated, one CTA.
- **Proven:** the most-repeated long-running template in the vertical (running since early-mid 2025).
- **Best for:** every client, especially as the static or solution-aware ad. Strongest in a
  clearly named local area.
- **Adapt:** no emojis in the checklist (plain short lines). The CTA is the free consult, never a
  "$49, 30 spots" voucher.

### 14. Practitioner talking to camera (60-90 seconds)
- **What:** the owner or PT speaking straight to camera, native and unpolished.
- **Proven:** dominated the highest-impression PT and chiro ads in the September scan.
- **Best for:** clients with an owner who's comfortable on camera and has a story (the in-house
  pillar from the AdsMastery 2027 section). Becomes the founder-story or full-structure video
  from the Creative Don video section.
- **Adapt:** raw educational clips need an ad structure (hook, mechanism, review proof, CTA) before
  they're usable. If the client has no footage, an AI UGC actor can voice the situation, but never
  as a patient.

## Static formats (added 2026-10-08, from the static ads masterclass)

Any concept above can be made in several static formats. Taking one winning message into a new
format is the cheapest creative diversity. Pick a format *and* a production level (graphic, hi-fi,
or **lo-fi phone-photo look**, which is underused and working now).

| Format | PT version | Best concepts to pair with |
|---|---|---|
| Educational infographic | "3 reasons your knee aches after long runs"; "What happens in your first visit, step by step" | #6, #7, #8, #12 (unaware / problem aware) |
| Headliner | One big line, e.g. "Most clinics book you for an hour. You see the PT for 15 minutes." (only if true locally, never naming a competitor) | #7, #4, taboo angles |
| Benefit callouts | A real session photo with 3 callouts max (one-on-one hour, in-home option, a plan you can follow) | #2, #9, #13 |
| Comparison | One-on-one PT vs. rest / chiro / injections / the 15-minute clinic | #4, #9, #12 (solution aware) |
| Transformation | A functional before/after told through a real review ("couldn't lift my daughter" → "carried her up the stairs"). No body images | #6, #8, #10 (problem / solution aware). The biggest gap in most accounts |
| Grid | "What we help with," or the team | #13. Lowest priority for PT |
| Text-only | A founder letter from the PT ("Why I left the big clinic to open my own"), notes-app style | #14, in-house pillar |

## Seen but excluded

- **Results headlines** ("12 years of edema swelling gone in 2 days", "Watch this patient's
  story"): medical claims plus an implied condition. These break Meta's health rules and our
  no-fabrication rule. Real review cards do this job instead.
- **Price + scarcity vouchers** ("$49 intro, only 30 spots"): break the client-funnel default (free
  consult, never a price), and contradict no-pressure brands. Allowed only when `build-offer`
  produced an approved paid offer and the scarcity is the client's real capacity.

## Choosing concepts for a client (how the agent uses this file)

1. Read the client's `client-context.md`, onboarding (services, age range, differentiators) and
   `deep-research.md` (segments, pains, insider words).
2. **Filter:** drop concepts the client can't honestly run (no home visits → skip #9; no pelvic PT →
   skip #11; no workshop host → skip #1; age cap under 65 → skip or soften #10).
3. **Match to segments:** for each target segment, pick the concepts whose "best for" fits it.
   E.g. desk workers 30-50 → #6, #7, #13. Runners → #8, #3, #12. Women 45+ → #5, #11, #6.
   Seniors → #10, #4, #1.
4. **Spread across stages and formats:** cover the ladder (AdsMastery 2027 section) and the six
   formats (review, benefit, founder, educate, problem, offer). Never two concepts that are the same
   concept with a cosmetic change.
5. **Pair each chosen concept with a proven ad** (P# from `context/proven-ads/`) for structure,
   and with a research quote for the hook. Show the picks and the reasons at the concepts gate
   (G7) for Dylan to approve.

Add new concepts here as they're found (new dated entry under the right section). Client-specific
results ("#9 won for Crosacore") go in that client's `client-context.md`, not here.
