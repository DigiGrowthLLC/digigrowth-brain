## 1. Segment snapshot

**Neck pain + headaches.** Adults 30-80, desk/phone workers, drivers, lifters, people on their feet all day (a tattoo artist, a stylist, construction). The pain lives "between the shoulder blades", in the "rhomboid", "traps", "back of the head", often with tingling or numbness into the arm and fingers, and frequently with one-sided headaches they assumed were migraines. **Trigger moment:** a night they can't sleep ("it's 3am and my left side of neck and shoulder are killing me" [N5]; "sitting up with an ice pack on my head and heating pad on my neck" at 3:30 a.m. [M-on2]), or a tingling arm that scares them into searching (N2 "started to tingle. A Google search led me to the possibility of a pinched nerve").

**Sciatica / nerve pain.** Adults 30-80; the older half (55+) is the most desperate and the most treated (injections, fusions, gabapentin). Pain "from the lower back to the calf", "my right side is on fire" [S1], "the worst zing in your butt cheeks" [M-sci]. **Trigger moment:** a flare that takes away sitting, driving or work ("I am truck driver and I can't even work" [S3]; "hrs of sitting in my truck to the point where I can walk across my living room but thats it" [M-sci]), or lying flat on the floor for days (Tiara "lying flat on a hard floor for 3 days" [AT-card]; "I couldn't even move ... lay flat from like Saturday afternoon until about Monday morning" [AT-reel]).

**Failed other care.** People from both segments who are months or years in, with a list: PT (often twice), chiro, massage, injections, pills, sometimes surgery. **Trigger moment:** relief wearing off again, or being told the next step is another shot or surgery ("was getting shots into that upper hip area every few months, walk good for awhile then start giving back the gains" [M-sci]; "So Monday I saw the spine doc who said I need surgery and then that afternoon I saw this other doctor that said I do not" [M-c56]).

## 2. Pains, ranked

1. **Relief that doesn't last / it keeps coming back.** The most-repeated pain in the failed-care set.
   - "I suffer this problem so many months go chiropractor so many times it's helped but still comes back" [S1]
   - "Lots of treatment and it went away till last week where it came back with a vengeance." [ST]
   - "walk good for awhile then start giving back the gains." [M-sci]
2. **Sleep.** The #1 daily cost for neck patients, close behind sitting for sciatica.
   - "last night I slept pain free for the first time in 2 years" [N2]
   - "I just slept my second night completely pain free on my shoulder after almost 3 months not sleeping well ... I even had a dream last night." [N3]
   - "would have to sleep upright in my recliner with my head gently resting against ice" [M-on]
3. **Can't sit / drive / work.**
   - "31 year old construction worker. I have been in so much pain while driving and when trying to return from a seated position." [S2]
   - "It is hard to sit without pain." [M-sci]; "For several years I carried a pillow to sit on wherever I went." [M-sci]
4. **Nerve symptoms that frighten them** (tingling, numb fingers, burning).
   - "The pain and tingling travels down my arm to the elbow." [M-sb]
   - "The burning is what brought me here today." [N4]; "Sometimes it burns so bad and I can't stand the pain" [N1]
5. **Emotional wear: hopelessness and being alone with it.**
   - "I'm actually losing hope it feels like my skull is literally coming off my spine" [N1]
   - "Its sad how eventually you just accept the pain as part of your life." [M-sci]
   - "the pain is killing me and my mental health too." [S3]

## 5. Objections and fears

- **Cost / cash-pay** (log for build-offer; never answered with a price in ads): "Too much of a poor college student to afford to go see someone" [S2]; "I was about to spend a lot of money and go to physical therapy ... I immediately canceled my appointment" [N3]; "she charges $160/session and does not take insurance ... this is not within my budget" [M-on]; "useless and expensive!" [M-sci].
- **Insurance** (expectation that care is covered): "the Mayo Clinic does not accept my insurance" [M-sb]; "waiting on insurance now to approve an MRI because they originally wanted me to do six months of physical therapy" [N1]; "medicare has cut my time to everyfours months" [M-on2].
- **"PT didn't work last time"**: "PT actually exacerbated symptoms" [M-sci]; "prescribed physical therapy which I had already been doing for years" [M-on].
- **Surgery and injections**: see section 3; "The more I read up on cervical spine surgery the more I am not sure I want it." [M-c56].
- **Not being believed / bounced around**: "I tried to explain my pain to doctors, physical therapists, etc, and I never felt fully understood" [N3]; "I've been bounced around from specialist to specialist" [H1]; "I will go to specialist after specialist if I have to until I feel like I am being heard" [M-c56].
- **DIY first**: a large share of the YouTube audience tries free fixes before paying anyone ("No doctors, no painkillers and no exercises have worked as effectively and simply as this set" [S2]). Implication: cheap leads include DIYers; keep a qualifier.

## 8. Emotional register

- **Lead element: Emotion for failed-care, Curiosity for neck-headache, Connection for sciatica-active.**
  - Failed-care is dark: frustration, resignation, being unheard ("eventually you just accept the pain" [M-sci]; "the goal post keeps moving away from me" [M-on2]; "never felt fully understood" [N3]). Lead with validation, then the turn ("finally had some hope" [AT-funnel]).
  - Neck-headache is largely unaware that the neck is involved ("I truly never thought it could be related to my spine" [M-on]; "I thought my one-sided headaches were migraines all this time" [H1]). A mechanism reveal (Curiosity) fits.
  - Sciatica in active people is identity-driven (CrossFit competitor, acrobat, "squat and deadlift pain-free") [AT-card][S2]: Connection via their world.
- **Dark vs light:** dark (frustration, fear of surgery, tired of pills) for failed-care and older sciatica; lighter, identity/humor for the gym crowd ("Doing God's work" style gratitude is common, but avoid testimonial-speak from non-patients).
