## 1. Segment snapshot

**A: the active adult (about 28-60).** They run, ski, climb, ride or lift, and the sport is part of who they are. The problem is rarely one acute injury. It's the same knee, hip, back or shoulder that flares every season. They've done PT, massage and foam rolling, and they "push through" until they can't. *Trigger:* a race, ski season or a trip coming up (MEND mountain-bike trip, NBPT Camino, V6 "4 wks before ski trip", ALT "back on the slopes before the season ended"), or being told to stop (T6 "if it hurts you probably just can't do it anymore").

**B: the tried-everything chronic case (about 35-70).** Pain for years, in the neck/shoulder, low back, hip or knee. They have a list of everything they've tried and can recite it (T2, T4, V7, CL Annette). Relief lasts "a day or two" (T3). Scans come back "normal" (T3, V1). They're tired, sometimes tearful (V1 ×4). *Trigger:* the last thing they tried just faded, or the next step on offer is an injection or surgery they don't want (RA, T4, V5).

## 2. Pains, ranked

   - "Given me stretches, exercises etc ..all help but don't fix the problem" (T2)

   - "I tried dry needling for a while, and it helped a little bit, but the pain always came back." (RK)
   - "I learned to manage the pain but it didn't go away." (RA)
2. **Losing the activity, and the identity that goes with it (A).**
   - "I'm only 40 and too young to give up on my passion." (T6)
   - "its been 3 years since I've been able to run more than 2 miles." (V2)
   - "I've gone from playing four hours of pickleball in the morning with an afternoon hike last year to 500 steps of agony" (V5)
   - "I still climb but not as hard as I used to." (V4)
3. **Pain running the whole day (B).**
   - "I basically have back pain 24/7; from the time I'm awake in the morning, while at the bus stop, at work, and even lying in bed" (T3)
   - "I don't remember a morning I haven't woken up with a stiff back" (T2)

4. **Nobody can say what's actually wrong.**
   - "it's always the same story, "everything looks fine, everything's normal"." (T3)
   - "I saw two sports ortho docs who specialize in knees, and they weren't able to tell me anything much." (ST)
   - "Physical therapy three times without a diagnosis" (V4)
5. **Fear of making it worse or of it flaring again.**

## 5. Objections and fears
- **Price vs. uncertainty:** "$155 a session is pretty pricey when the outcome is uncertain." (MP-MAT); "felt like my care wasn't good enough to justify the cost" (V4); "I am spending a lot of money PT, physio, msk doctor but nothing" (V1). → The $49 first visit lowers this risk. Note for `build-offer`: the fear is paying for another thing that doesn't work, not the price itself.
- **"Is it only short-term, like massage?"** (MP-MAT). This is the #1 MAT-specific objection, and Aaron's why-you answers it directly (OB "Not just symptom relief that lasts a day or a week").
- **Skepticism of unfamiliar methods:** "it's not uncommon for doctors and PTs to roll their eyes at MAT" (RA); "A person who has lost hope with real doctors is super easy to convince" (T3). → Lead with DPT credentials and a test-retest people can see, not with the MAT name.
- **Upsells / forever visits:** "tried to sell me on herbal supplements" (MP-MAT); "working towards a point where I didn't need to be seeing them" (T3, praise for physios). → Careful: Aaron's model is maintenance visits. Don't promise "no more visits" in ads.
- **Being dismissed:** "She gaslit me ... saying my pain was all in my head" (ALT 1★); "This is not in your head" (T4).
- **Being told to stop:** T6 (ortho); V4 "Should I stop climbing"; V1 "no bending, twisting or lifting, there went my hobbies".
- **Insurance:** only one thread (T1 "don't have insurance"). Boulder cash-pay tolerance looks high (OB), but it's thinly evidenced.

## 6. Language bank
**Self-labels:** "longtime runner", "avid runner and athlete", "avid lifter", "skeptic" / "perpetually skeptical", "tried everything", "living with it", "broken".
**Their words for the problem:** "comes and goes", "flares back up", "sneak back", "my back goes out", "throw my back out", "locks up", "seizing up", "couldn't kick", "same old", "hit a wall", "push through".
**Phrases to lift (adapt, never present as a testimonial):**
- "all help but don't fix the problem"
- "feel good for a day or two"
- "everything looks fine, everything's normal"
- "too young to give up on" [running / skiing]
- "a sheet of exercises" / "cookie cutter"
- "it can feel like your on an assembly line"
- "smaller muscles that have stopped doing their job" (best plain-English MAT line)
- "a seemingly unrelated muscle", "re-test", "marked changes"
- "I learned to manage the pain but it didn't go away"
**Stock phrases to avoid** (everyone says them, and readers have stopped seeing them): "root cause", "get back to doing what you love", "holistic", "personalized care", "unlock your body's potential" (on Aaron's own site), "pain-free life".

## 7. Competitor gaps (Boulder)
- **The market is crowded and highly rated:** the top 10 PT clinics are all 4.9-5.0 with 140-717 reviews. Aaron (solo, small review count) loses any star-rating comparison, so his ads shouldn't ask for one.
- **What competitors win on:** getting people back to sport before a deadline (ALT, MEND, NBPT), listening (MEND, NBPT), whole-body testing (MEND), and easy scheduling (NBPT). Their norm is about 12 visits with home exercise programs (NBPT).
- **Gaps Aaron can credibly own:**
  1. **Same hands, the whole hour, every visit.** Bigger clinics rotate therapists (ALT has multiple PTs per patient; T5 "assembly line"). Aaron is a solo DPT (true per OB/funnel).
  2. **Different from what you've already tried.** Every competitor is "more PT plus exercises". Aaron's mechanism (find the muscle that stopped working, test, re-test) is distinct (CL Lisa "I was able to see how my body reacted differently"; MP-MAT test-retest).
  3. **For the person PT already didn't fix.** "countless physical therapists" (CL Max), "more improvement than months of PT" (CL Nicolene), "stumped dozens of other medical professionals" (CL Amie). Competitors can't make this claim; Aaron's own reviews make it for him.
  4. **Not in your head.** ALT 1★, T4.

## 8. Emotional register
- **Segment A: lead with Connection plus Emotion, light-to-dark.** Identity ("longtime runner", "too young to give up") and a deadline (ski season now). Some frustration, but these people are hopeful and action-minded. Evidence: T6, V2, MEND, ALT, V6.
- **Segment B: lead with Emotion (dark), then Curiosity.** Fatigue and frustration, "tried everything" lists, temporary relief. Then a curiosity turn: "the sore spot isn't the problem / a muscle that stopped doing its job." Evidence: T2, T3, V1, V7, CL Annette/Gabby/Nicolene. Advantage's winning ads (C7/C4/C3, $10-14 CPL) used exactly this dark-then-curiosity shape.
- Avoid pity and despair visuals. Don't use "broken".
