Samantha's story

Samantha became a physical therapist because of her own high school PT, who explained her hypermobile joints, treated her never-ending injuries and kept her in the game.

After Calvin College (kinesiology, psychology and French), she began her Doctorate of Physical Therapy in 2016. Her education came to an abrupt halt when being a "practice patient" in labs caused multiple joint injuries, treatments that were contraindicated for her hypermobility. She became the first student in the USA to transfer to a new DPT program, and graduated in 2022. That experience is why she believes patients know their bodies best, and need to be believed.

Why traditional PT often fails EDS patients

The insurance-driven model schedules 2-3 short visits a week, often under half an hour, then hands patients to a PT tech. That works for a simple post-surgery rehab. It doesn't work for EDS, where bodies are complex, don't tolerate standard protocols, and need dedicated one-on-one time.

Many physical therapists also start with strengthening and stabilizing, which can cause more pain and injury in hypermobile patients. Movement re-training, muscle control and coordination have to come first.

How Samantha treats EDS

  • No protocols, including the Muldowney protocol. Every plan is individualized.
  • Control first, then strengthened stability, one-on-one and never rushed.
  • Traditional PT combined with somatic and nervous system re-education.

Why hypermobile joints hurt

Chronic pain develops when there's excess movement in hypermobile joints, or when fragile connective tissue has had previous or unresolved injuries. Over time, repeated stretching causes small micro-tears that lead to pain and inflammation.

Control first, then strength

It's important for hypermobile people to get strong and stay strong, but that's often impossible without pain when strengthening comes first. Samantha starts with movement re-training and muscle control, then builds strengthened stability, one-on-one in a non-rushed environment.

Is this chronic pain?

Chronic pain can be dull, aching, throbbing, burning, sharp or shooting, and it often gets in the way of things you want or need to do every day. A few questions to ask yourself:

  • Has physical therapy in the past not changed it, or made it worse?
  • Does pain stop you from doing daily or favorite activities?
  • Do people tell you the pain doesn't match how you look?

A gentle, believed approach

Even when the original injury is gone, the body and brain can keep a pattern where simple movements hurt. Samantha listens to your story, believes your pain, and uses gentle, gradual methods to change that pattern, never pushing past what your body can take.

Frequently asked questions

Do you use the Muldowney Protocol?+

No. Samantha uses many varied approaches and complex problem-solving, and there are no protocols at this clinic. She has worked with patients, including herself, who tried that protocol without solving their pain.

Do I need a referral from my doctor?+

Referrals are not required. Insurance will require a prescription before it pays.

Do you use PTAs or PT techs?+

No. Samantha is the only clinician right now, and every visit is one-on-one with her.

Do you take out-of-state patients?+

Yes. Patients come from anywhere. If you have nationwide insurance, coverage can be verified before you come. The clinic is about 10 minutes from motels and restaurants.
