# Funnel & Landing Page Best Practices

## General High-Converting Landing Page Principles

- **Single focused intent.** One conversion goal per page, not multiple competing
  CTAs. Single-offer pages convert at 3-5x the rate of homepage-directed traffic.
- **Structure that works, in order:** benefit-led headline above the fold -> a value
  proposition under 30 words -> 3-5 proof points -> social proof/testimonial -> one
  primary CTA -> (if a form is needed at all) 3 fields or fewer.
- **Headline answers "why should I care?"** within the first few seconds — lead with
  the benefit, not the mechanism.
- **Mobile-first is the default, not an afterthought** — 60%+ of traffic is mobile.
  Load time matters concretely: each additional second of load time costs ~7% conversion.
- **Minimize form friction.** Every extra field loses respondents. Ask only what's
  needed to follow up; gather more later. Progressive disclosure can lift completion
  30-50% when a form is unavoidable.
- **CTA language should match funnel stage** — soft ("Learn More", "See how it works")
  for cold/top-of-funnel; direct ("Book a Call", "Grab a Spot") for warm/bottom-of-funnel.
  A cold-outreach mockup page is top-of-funnel — keep the CTA low-pressure.

## Conversion Psychology / Copywriting

- **Curiosity gap:** tease a specific, real gap between what the reader knows and
  wants to know — enough to pull them forward, without giving the payoff away
  immediately. Works for headlines, preview text, and OG link-preview copy alike.
  Don't oversell it into clickbait — the gap has to resolve honestly on the page.
- **Trust signals beat self-claims.** Specifics (real names, real numbers, a real
  guarantee) outperform vague superlatives ("amazing results"). This matches
  DigiGrowth's own existing brand rule (see content-agent/context/brand-strategy.md)
  against vague claims — reinforce, don't contradict it.
- **Loss aversion / stakes framing** lands harder than pure upside framing for a
  skeptical, busy audience (independent practice owners) — e.g. "what happens to
  your schedule when your top referral source goes quiet" over generic upside-only
  pitches. (This mirrors what the SMS campaign report already found working in real
  conversations — pull-quote-style stakes framing, not a features list.)
- **Most decisions are subconscious/fast.** A skeptical prospect decides in seconds
  whether a page/link is worth their attention — the first visual impression (does
  this look real, does it look like *them*) does more work than the copy underneath it.
- **A real, specific guarantee outperforms a vague promise.** Anchor claims to
  DigiGrowth's actual stated guarantee (10-20 booked consultations in 6 weeks, or
  continued work for free) rather than invented industry statistics — this is also
  a hard compliance rule, not just a style preference.

## PT-Practice-Specific Patient Acquisition Funnel

- **The patient funnel, PT-specific:** Discovery -> Trust -> Conversion -> Commitment
  -> Referral (or, in the simpler framing: Awareness -> Consideration -> Conversion
  -> Loyalty). A cold-outreach mockup page sits at the very start (Discovery/Awareness)
  — its only job is to earn enough trust to get a reply/click, not to close anything.
- **Friction removal is the single biggest lever** in PT patient acquisition — an
  obvious booking option, a simple form, and a fast human response. "The clinic that
  replies first usually wins the patient" — directly relevant to the guarantee's own
  "how fast you follow up" pillar already in the current SMS sequence.
- **Dedicated landing pages outperform sending traffic to a homepage** for any
  single, specific offer — exactly the mechanic this skill is building (a page built
  for this one prospect, not a generic company homepage).
- **Referral-driven patients show up more and stay longer** than other acquisition
  channels — useful context for the page's funnel-explainer copy (why the guarantee
  focuses on consistent booked volume, not just "more leads").
- **Website speed and first impression matter disproportionately** in this vertical
  — 70% of consumers say site speed affects willingness to engage. Keep the generated
  mockup page lightweight (no heavy JS frameworks, inline critical CSS) — this is
  also just good practice for a page that needs to render fast from a cold SMS tap.

## Applying This to the `landing-page-mockup` Skill

- Top-of-page mockup section: benefit-led framing of *their* booking flow, personalized,
  low-friction (no form at all needed at this stage — the ask is "look," not "convert"
  yet). Their brand colors, not DigiGrowth's — this is "what yours could look like."
- Scroll-down funnel section: stakes-framed problem statement -> DigiGrowth's real
  guarantee (the actual number, not an invented industry stat) -> one clear, low-pressure CTA.
- Keep it short. This is a curiosity planter for a cold prospect, not a sales page for
  a warm lead — over-explaining the mechanism undercuts the curiosity gap and reads
  as trying too hard for a stranger's attention.
