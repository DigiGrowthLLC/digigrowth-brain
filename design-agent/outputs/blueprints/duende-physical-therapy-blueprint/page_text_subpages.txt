Why Duende?

Pronounced DWEHN-deh.

Duende is a Spanish word with many meanings, from mischievous spirits to an indefinable charm. But the inspiration for the name comes from the concept of duende in art: a raw, almost mystical force of emotion and authentic expression. Born from struggle, rooted in the soul, and capable of moving us in ways that words cannot.

For me, it's the passion and energy I bring to our sessions. It's the grit, determination, and vulnerable bravery my patients bring when they believe they can get better and insist on getting the care they deserve. Even after years of pain. Even after being dismissed or ignored by the medical system.

I first encountered the concept of duende through Federico García Lorca, a queer, anti-fascist poet and playwright of the early 20th century who delivered a lecture on the subject. I studied his plays in high school and later had the chance to explore my heritage and his poetry while studying abroad in his hometown of Granada, Spain.

Lorca was revolutionary in both his artistic techniques and his confrontation of class inequality, sexism, and sexual repression. He refused to hide his political views and brought theater to underserved communities. 

I'm a queer Latina who shares Lorca's commitment to challenging inequity. At Duende, I put that into practice through sliding-scale pricing, charitable giving, and dignified care for all.

Duende Physical Therapy is an ode to queer expression, rebellion against oppression, the precious brevity of life, and the stubborn, irrational hope that things can get better.

Putting Values Into Practice
Sustainability

Working in hospital systems, I saw firsthand how much waste healthcare produces. I wasn’t just a witness to it; I was complicit in it. One of my goals in opening Duende was to have more control over the materials I use and reduce my footprint on this planet.

At Duende, I use biodegradable gloves and trash bags, reusable absorbent bed pads, real linens, and recycled paper products. I minimize paper and plastic use where I can, and I consider sustainability when purchasing supplies for the clinic.

Equity

Fatphobia, racism, classism, sexism, and discrimination based on gender or sexuality are widespread in healthcare. They can make it harder to get care—and harder to feel cared for.

At Duende, I strive to make care welcoming and respectful for people of all races, body sizes, genders, sexualities, religions, cultures, incomes, and abilities. I provide trans-affirming, poly/ENM-friendly, sex worker–affirming, and sex-positive care. I respect religious and cultural practices, including preferences around modesty, touch, and what feels comfortable during an exam.

I offer sliding-scale appointments to help make care more accessible and do my best to accommodate your needs. If there’s something I can do better, I want to hear it.

Support

Where we spend our money matters. Whenever possible, I prioritize supporting women-, queer-, and POC-owned businesses, as well as local businesses and artists. 

Giving back is part of Duende, too. I donate 3% of profits to organizations working toward safer births, equal rights, and a healthier planet.

Birth Future Foundation

In the U.S., Black women are more than three times as likely to die from maternal causes as white women, and their babies are nearly 2.5 times as likely to die before their first birthday. This disparity is unacceptable.

Research has linked midwifery and doula support for Black mothers to better birth outcomes, including fewer cesarean deliveries, preterm births, and low-birth-weight babies. That's why I support Birth Future Foundation, which helps break down the financial barriers keeping Black, Indigenous, and other people of color from entering and thriving in the birth profession. We need more Black and Brown providers in the field, and more moms and babies coming home safely.
