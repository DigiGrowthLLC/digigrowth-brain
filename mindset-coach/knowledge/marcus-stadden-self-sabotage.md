# Self-Sabotage Framework (Marcus Stadden)

Core line: **self-sabotage is the binge at the end of a psychological crash diet.**

---

## 1. What self-sabotage is

- **Definition:** anything you do or don't do that breaks consistency toward a goal you *still
  want*, with nothing external stopping you. When actions fail to meet intentions, the gap between
  them is the sabotage.
- **Why it's priority #1:** consistency is the one unsexy thing behind every form of success
  (health, wealth, relationships). Skills, authority, knowledge and money only compound if
  consistency isn't interrupted. Self-sabotage interrupts it.

## 2. The two types

| Overt (tip of the iceberg) | Covert (most of the iceberg) |
|---|---|
| Obvious: the binge, the lost weekend, scrolling at 2 AM, a relapse | Subtle and normalized: procrastination, overthinking, "more research" |
| You *know* it's sabotage while you're doing it | It *feels productive*: you're working **around** the work, not on it |
| Comes with guilt | No guilt, which is what makes it more dangerous |

Covert examples: talking to ChatGPT about your business model for 2 hours with no action, shiny
object syndrome, half-built bridges, jumping ship as soon as something gets hard.
**Everyone has a part of them resisting every action toward the goal**, including people who think
"why would I sabotage something I want?"

## 3. The cycle of mediocrity

A wave with progress on the y-axis and time on the x-axis:
1. **Rock bottom (the trough):** a rut. A trigger (a photo, a bank balance, New Year, a birthday)
   makes **the pain of staying the same exceed the pain of change**.
2. **The climb:** lock in, build routines, drop bad habits. "This is it, I've cracked the code."
3. **The peak:** the honeymoon fades, the work gets heavier, complacency sets in (the desperation
   from the bottom is gone), standards slip.
4. **The crash:** not just no progress but **negative progress**, actively tearing down what you
   built (the dieter who binges worse than before the diet).
5. Back to rock bottom. Repeat.

- The **net progress line is flat** because compounding keeps getting interrupted. Action isn't
  saved in a bank account (4 weeks on, 4 weeks off = you look like you never went).
- The cycle can run in a day, a week, a month or years.
- It tracks your **comfort zone**: people bounce between their minimum and maximum standards.
- Eventually people stop trying and solidify into an identity (30s-50s).
- Governing law (Jeremy Bentham): **pain and pleasure govern human behavior.** We change when
  staying the same hurts more. We stop changing when changing hurts more.

## 4. The mechanism: resistance

- **Resistance = emotional friction toward goal-aligned action.**
- Two families: **fear-based** ("I'm scared to do the thing": fear, anxiety, guilt, shame) and
  **apathy-based** ("I can't be bothered": boredom, apathy).
- It acts like a **fog** over your mind. You can't make decisions in your future self's interest
  from inside fear or apathy.

### The sabotage loop (why one slip becomes a lost year)
Trigger → resistance (uncomfortable emotion) → **escape** via a coping mechanism (scrolling, food,
a night out, "another week of research") → **short-term relief** (it does work, briefly) →
**shame** (you know you dropped below who you can be) → shame is *more resistance* → repeat.
One bad day becomes a lost week, then a lost month, then a lost year.

**The fix for the loop: forgive yourself immediately.** Marcus's line: *"What happened happened,
and it couldn't have happened any other way, because it simply didn't."* Every moment is a fresh
slate. Be very good at messing up and forgiving yourself straight away. One bad day doesn't break
progress; the shame spiral does.

### Where resistance comes from: you
- We externalize it ("something happened, I'm stressed"), but the emotional response originates in
  your mind. **All resistance is self-generated.** The task didn't change, you did.
- Take radical responsibility for your feelings.
- It comes from mismanaging two parts of your psyche: **psychological needs** (starved) and
  **psychological values** (overridden). Usually both.

## 5. Psychological needs (universal): Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan)

"Psychological nutrients": everyone needs all three to thrive. When all three are met you get
**intrinsic motivation**.

| Need | Meaning | Starved when... | Cheap copy the "horse" reaches for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Competence** | Feeling your effort works and you're getting better (action linked to result) | 3 months of gym and you look the same; long lag between work and results | Video games (levelling up), anything with fake progress |
| **Autonomy** | Feeling your choices are your own; agency over time, place, choices | Every hour belongs to someone else's plan, even a copied 5 AM routine; working on someone else's mission | **Procrastination becomes the only choice that still feels like yours**; rebelling against your own plan |
| **Relatedness** | Belonging: you care about people and they care about you | Monk mode, building alone in your room, lone-wolf goals | Scrolling, stalking people online, fake connection |

**"Your sabotage is a cheap, fast copy of what your life is missing."**

### The rider and the horse (Freud)
- **Rider** = the thinking brain (prefrontal cortex): the plan, the to-do list, the goals.
- **Horse** = the unconscious: needs, drives, emotions. **Far stronger than the rider.**
- Feed the horse (meet your needs) and it runs willingly wherever you want. That's intrinsic
  motivation.
- Starve it and it produces resistance. Whip it ("f*** your feelings, work harder", caffeine,
  stimulants, more discipline) and eventually **it throws you off and runs to the nearest food**.
  That's the sabotage.
- "A starving horse will eat whatever's closest."
- Freud: *"If the rider doesn't want to be thrown, he is obliged to guide the horse where it wants
  to go."*
- Discipline alone is not enough.

## 6. Psychological values (personal): Shalom Schwartz's values wheel

Values are **core enduring beliefs about what matters most to you**, an internal north star. Needs
are biology (the same for everyone); values are personality (unique). Most people have 3-4 main
values.

| Quadrant | Values |
|---|---|
| Openness to change | **Self-direction** (run your own life, agency), **Stimulation** (novelty, excitement, challenge), **Hedonism** (enjoying life) |
| Self-enhancement | **Achievement** (succeeding, proving competence), **Power** (status, control over people and resources) |
| Conservation | **Security** (safety, stability, predictability), **Conformity** (not letting people down, fitting the mold), **Tradition** (respecting the customs you grew up with) |
| Self-transcendence | **Benevolence** (care for the people close to you), **Universalism** (fairness and care for everyone and nature) |

Values on opposite sides of the wheel conflict. A therapist friend of Marcus: **9 times out of 10,
people's problems are values conflicts.**

Overriding a value creates **cognitive dissonance** (actions vs beliefs). Every day the goal
overrides a value, resistance gets louder until behavior gives way. A goal that fights your values
can only be pushed by "I should", and **living by "should" starves autonomy too.**

### The 4 most common conflicts
1. **Achievement vs Hedonism:** 6 weeks all-in with no nights out. The pleasure you cut doesn't
   disappear; it waits.
2. **Achievement vs Benevolence:** every hour on the goal feels taken from people you care about.
3. **Self-direction vs Security:** one foot in each. You never fully commit to the side project and
   never fully settle in the job.
4. **Self-direction vs Conformity (the most common):** you want your own path, but the people you
   love want you on theirs (the "go to university and become a doctor" pressure).

### Psychological starvation
Chasing a goal with a routine that starves your needs or overrides your values. Willpower is finite;
hunger grows daily. Where the two lines cross is **the peak of the cycle**. It works like a crash
diet: strong start → growing hunger → binge → guilt → spiral. **The harder you lock in on a routine
that starves you, the faster the hunger builds.** By the peak, most of what you feel as "the pain of
change" is psychological hunger. The elastic band snaps back.

## 7. The solution: psychological nutrition

Design your goal and routine so they **feed your needs and values**. Then nothing in you is starving
enough to break out. It's the difference between a crash diet and a way of eating you can keep for
life. **Build a road the horse loves instead of forcing it down one it hates.**

### Step 1: find your values (combine several sources; there's no definitive list)
- The **Schwartz Value Survey** online (~20 questions).
- **Q1:** If you had unlimited money and nobody to impress (a desert island, all resources), how
  would you spend your days?
- **Q2:** If failure and embarrassment were impossible, what would you go after?
- **Q3:** What reliably annoys you in other people? Judgement is a projection: it points to a value
  you hold (Marcus: incompetence annoys him → he values competence).
- **Read your sabotage:** your coping mechanisms reveal what you're starving (scrolling and stalking
  → connection; gaming → self-direction/progression; drinking → pleasure/stimulation).
- **Check against reality:** look at last month's calendar. What does it say you prioritize? Write
  down 2-3 recurring patterns and ask what they give you that a normal week doesn't.

### Step 2: feed your needs on purpose (treat them like sleep: non-negotiable)
- **Competence: shorten the feedback loop.** Track the inputs you control, chase small personal
  bests, keep some work where effort shows up within days. Habit tracker, celebrate small wins,
  process over outcome, inputs over outputs. "If you're doing the right things, it's a matter of
  time."
- **Autonomy: own the how.** Build your own routine even if you started from someone else's. Where
  are you copying someone, where are you being inauthentic? Spend time alone (a cabin in the middle
  of nowhere); within a couple of weeks you drop the act and see who you really are.
- **Relatedness: the most neglected one.** Get around people who share your ambitions, ideally
  **people chapters ahead of you** (if you're at chapter 4, find people at 6, 7, 8). The opportunity
  cost of being around the wrong people is huge. Social media is not enough. Take the leap and
  introduce yourself; making friends isn't that hard.

### Step 3: feed your values (two lines of defense)
1. **Synergize: find the Goldilocks zone** (projects and people that feed several values at once).
   - Achievement + Benevolence → teaching what you're learning.
   - Achievement + Hedonism → training with friends.
   - *"There are a million ways to make a million dollars. You don't need to pick one that makes
     you miserable."* Most successful people's regret is *how* they got there (survival mode,
     scarcity, desperation). Achieving the goal slower but aligned beats being miserable now to be
     happy later.
2. **Seasons:** when values truly clash, pick a **lead value** for a season (months or years) with a
   purpose and an end date.
   - **Never breach the floor:** every other value still gets a minimum weekly dose (dinner with
     friends, a lazy Sunday, visiting home).
   - **Ban a value completely and it breaks back in sideways** (monk mode → pleasure returns as a
     binge). Rigid all-or-nothing dieters binge more often and more severely than flexible ones.
   - **Make the lead value the vehicle for the ones on hold:** achievement now → more resources
     for benevolence and enjoyment later. That turns sacrifice into **a trade with an end date**.
   - A jack of all trades is a master of none: focus matters.

### Consistency over intensity
- **Judge your routine by the year, not the day.** People optimize for how much they can do in a
  day and get nothing done in a year (feast/famine).
- The monk-mode week feels like progress, and it's the week that starves you fastest.
- **Dials, not switches:** think in percentages, not on/off.
- The test question: **"Is this routine sustainable a year from now? Will I still be running it?"**
- Resistance will still show up and some weeks will still be hard, but a routine that feeds you
  doesn't build the hunger that ends in a crash.
